\title{Recovering a Core Allocation of the Mixed-Integer Community Game\\
via a Lagrangian Dual of the Copositive Lift}
\author{Working note}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We give a clean account of the following construction. The mixed-integer community
game is lifted to a completely positive program (CPP), exact for $\vmip$. The inter-player
structure is a family of \emph{homogeneous} (zero right-hand side) linear couplings ---
community balance together with symmetric-reserve adequacy and community peak-power
definition --- over player-separable mixed-integer feasible sets, plus a small set of
shared \emph{common variables} (the reserve and peak quantities) that every coalition
controls but no player owns, in the sense of linear production games with common
technology. We dualize the two families of
constraints of the CPP that carry the cross-player coupling at the lifted level --- the
joint completely positive cone and the squared (RLT) form of the couplings --- while
keeping the linear couplings and the per-player \emph{affine} constraints explicit. We
show, by explicit computation, that at any dual-feasible multiplier the resulting
Lagrangian is a \emph{block-separable linear production game} (LPG) with common technology,
with no surviving inter-player term in its value: the prosumer--prosumer \emph{and}
prosumer--common cross blocks vanish, the latter by the same mechanism, and --- because
every coupling has zero right-hand side --- no coalition-independent constant appears (a
nonzero right-hand side would break the LPG, which we show is the sharp dividing line). An
LPG has a nonempty core, and a fixed-multiplier Lagrangian is a coalition-wise upper bound
on $\vmip$; by the (profit form of the) Liu et al.\ composition, the LPG core allocation
is then a core allocation of the mixed-integer game.
We are careful about the duality hypotheses. The zero duality gap is automatic here (the
feasible region is bounded), which already yields, for every $\varepsilon>0$, a stable
allocation overpaying $\vmip$ by at most $\varepsilon$ --- an $\varepsilon$-core. An
\emph{exact} core requires in addition that the copositive dual optimum be \emph{attained};
following Cifuentes, Dey and Xu we note this does \emph{not} follow from boundedness, so we
treat it as a genuine hypothesis (the same one left implicit in existing copositive-pricing
work). We had hoped to discharge it structurally, but show that the natural route is closed:
Burer-type lifts have no primal interior point once binaries are present, so no
trade-structure assumption can supply a primal Slater point, and attainment remains an
instance-dependent condition --- equivalently, the condition under which a copositive
cutting-plane solver terminates. Because copositive
optimization is \textsf{NP}-hard, we claim an existence statement, not a computational
procedure. A separate section records a companion observation: if instead one drops the
squared coupling and the joint cone and lifts \emph{only} the per-player feasible sets to
their completely positive cones, the resulting program is exactly convex hull pricing ---
the community-balance LP with each $\feas_i$ replaced by $\conv(\feas_i)$ --- which connects
the present construction to the convex-hull-pricing allocation of the companion manuscript.
\end{abstract}

\section{The mixed-integer game and its copositive lift}

\paragraph{Players, shared common variables, and homogeneous couplings.}
Let $\N=\{1,\dots,n\}$ be the prosumers --- the \emph{players} of the game. Player $i$ has
variables $x_i=(\xcom_i,\xrest_i)$: $\xcom_i$ are the continuous \emph{coupling-relevant}
coordinates (those appearing in the inter-player constraints) and $\xrest_i$ collects all
remaining variables, including the binaries; $\feas_i$ is mixed-integer and bounded.
Separately, the dispatch carries a small block of \emph{common variables} $\xcommon\ge0$
that belong to no individual prosumer but are chosen by whichever coalition operates ---
in the model, the symmetric reserve quantity $r^{\mathrm{sym}}$ and the community peak
power $p$. These are \emph{not} a player: they are common decision variables that each
coalition $S$ controls freely within its own program (a coalition may pick a different
$\xcommon$), exactly the ``common technology'' of linear production games
(\citealp{Owen1975}; \citealp{Uhan2015}). They carry no characteristic-function value of
their own and receive no allocation; they appear only as shared columns in every
coalition's program, with objective coefficient $w_0$ (here
$w_0^{\top}\xcommon=\delta^{\mathrm{res}}r^{\mathrm{sym}}-\delta^{\mathrm{peak}}p$).

The inter-player constraints are a family of \emph{homogeneous} (zero right-hand side)
linear couplings, written compactly as
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i\in\N}\Acom_i\xcom_i\ +\ \Azero\,\xcommon\ =\ 0 ,
\label{eq:coupling}
\end{equation}
where each row of \eqref{eq:coupling} is one carrier--period balance, reserve-adequacy,
or peak-definition constraint. The right-hand side is $0$ for every row --- a fact we use
repeatedly (it is what keeps the relaxed game a production game,
Remark~\ref{rem:homogeneous}). The coalition value of the mixed-integer game
$\Gamma^{\mathrm{MIP}}$ is, in the profit (maximization) convention,
\begin{equation}
\vmip(S)=\max\Big\{\textstyle\sum_{i\in S} w_i^{\top}x_i+w_0^{\top}\xcommon :
\sum_{i\in S}\Acom_i\xcom_i+\Azero\xcommon=0,\ \xcommon\ge0,\ x_i\in \feas_i\ \forall i\in S\Big\}.
\label{eq:vmip}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The three coupling families (community balance, reserve, peak).}
Concretely the rows of \eqref{eq:coupling} are the following, all homogeneous; we keep
inequalities as written and convert them to equalities by nonnegative slacks in Step~1,
which does not change their zero right-hand side.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{Community balance} (per carrier $k\in\{E,G,H\}$, period $t$):
$\sum_{i\in S}\big(i^{\mathrm{com},k}_{it}-e^{\mathrm{com},k}_{it}\big)=0$. The coupling
coordinates here are the community import/export flows, with no common-variable term
($\Azero$ row $=0$).
\item \emph{Reserve adequacy} (per period $t$; up and down): the sold symmetric reserve
cannot exceed the coalition's aggregate offered headroom,
$r^{\mathrm{sym}}-\sum_{i\in S}\sum_k r^{k,+}_{it}\le0$ and
$r^{\mathrm{sym}}-\sum_{i\in S}\sum_k r^{k,-}_{it}\le0$. The common variable
$r^{\mathrm{sym}}$ enters with coefficient $+1$ (in $\Azero$); the prosumer reserve
offers $r^{k,\pm}_{it}$ (private to $i$, hence components of $\xcom_i$) with coefficient
$-1$.
\item \emph{Peak definition} (per period $t$, electricity):
$\sum_{i\in S}\tfrac{1}{\Delta T}\big(i^{E,\mathrm{mkt}}_{it}-e^{E,\mathrm{mkt}}_{it}\big)-p\le0$.
The common variable $p$ enters with coefficient $-1$ (in $\Azero$); the prosumer
grid-exchange variables with coefficient $\pm1/\Delta T$.
\end{itemize}
Every prosumer-private bound that supports these (e.g.\ the storage power/energy headroom
limits $r^{k,+}_{it}\le\bar b^{k,\mathrm{dis}}_i-b^{k,\mathrm{dis}}_{it}$ etc.) involves
\emph{only} player $i$'s own variables and is absorbed into $\feas_i$; it does not couple
players. The objective gains the common-variable term
$w_0^{\top}\xcommon=\delta^{\mathrm{res}}r^{\mathrm{sym}}-\delta^{\mathrm{peak}}p$; as
Section~\ref{sec:vanish} shows, the sign of $w_0$ is irrelevant to the reduction.

\paragraph{What is in $\feas_i$.}
The per-prosumer feasible region
\begin{equation}
\feas_i=\big\{x_i=(\xcom_i,\xrest_i):\ \text{prosumer balance, conversion, capacity,
storage, reserve-headroom, unit-commitment constraints}\big\}
\label{eq:feasi}
\end{equation}
is mixed-integer, bounded, and involves \emph{only} prosumer $i$'s own variables; the
coupling coordinates $\xcom_i$ now collect, over the horizon, the community import/export
flows, the reserve offers $r^{k,\pm}_{it}$, and the market grid-exchange variables
$i^{E,\mathrm{mkt}}_{it},e^{E,\mathrm{mkt}}_{it}$ --- exactly those touched by
\eqref{eq:coupling}. The coalition problem is
\begin{equation}
\vmip(S)=\max_{\substack{\{x_i\}_{i\in S}\\ \xcommon\ge0}}\Big\{\textstyle\sum_{i\in S}w_i^{\top}x_i+w_0^{\top}\xcommon\ :\
\underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{i\in S}\Acom_i\xcom_i+\Azero\xcommon=0}_{\text{couples players, shares common vars}},\ \
\underbrace{x_i\in\feas_i\ \forall i\in S}_{\text{per-player}}\Big\}.
\label{eq:vmip-struct}
\end{equation}
This separation --- a family of \emph{homogeneous} linear couplings over
player-separable feasible sets, plus a small block of shared common variables --- is the
structure we preserve through the lift. The baseline of earlier sections is the special
case $\xcommon=0$, $\Azero=0$, $w_0=0$ (no common variables) with a single balance row;
nothing below depends on that restriction, so we carry the general
\eqref{eq:vmip-struct}.

\begin{remark}[Common variables as ``common technology,'' not a player]
\label{rem:common-tech}
The variables $\xcommon$ are exactly the ``common technology'' of linear production games
in the sense of \citet{Owen1975} as generalized by \citet{Uhan2015}: shared decision
columns entering every coalition's program identically (no $i$-index), with their own
objective $w_0$, but with \emph{no} standalone coalition and \emph{no} allocated share.
They are controlled by whichever coalition operates, and the core allocation is computed
over the prosumers $\N$ only (Lemma~\ref{lem:owen}); $\xcommon$ never receives a payoff.
At the level of the copositive lift, $\xcommon$ does contribute a lifted diagonal block
$X_{00}$ and prosumer--common cross blocks $X_{i0}$ --- these are matrix bookkeeping, not
a player --- and we verify in Section~\ref{sec:vanish} that the cross blocks $X_{i0}$
vanish by the same mechanism as the prosumer--prosumer ones, so the common variables enter
the value only through their own block. The only difference from a model without common
variables is that $\Azero,w_0$ are no longer zero; the core argument is unchanged.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Step 1: equality form in the $x$-space, $\feaseq_i$.}
Following the standard preprocessing \citep[][\S4.1]{GuoEtAl}, applied \emph{within each
player block}, we put $\feas_i$ in Burer's mixed-binary form. Each binary
$z\in\{0,1\}$ (a component of $\xrest_i$) is rewritten with a slack $\sigma\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
z\in\{0,1\}
\ \Longleftrightarrow\
z-z^{2}=0,\ z\le 1
\ \Longleftrightarrow\
z-z^{2}=0,\ z+\sigma=1,\ z,\sigma\ge0,
\label{eq:binstd}
\end{equation}
and every inequality of \eqref{eq:feasi} is likewise made an equality by a nonnegative
slack. Writing $x_i\ge0$ for the resulting (slacked) vector and $z_i=(z_{i,m})_{m\in
B_i}$ for its binary sub-vector ($B_i$ the binary indices of player $i$), the
\emph{linear part} defines a set in the $x$-space,
\begin{equation}
\feaseq_i:=\big\{x_i\ge0:\ A_ix_i=b_i\big\},
\label{eq:Xeq}
\end{equation}
where $A_ix_i=b_i$ gathers all per-player linear equalities (including the slack
identities $z+\sigma=1$). The binary identities $z_{i,m}-z_{i,m}^{2}=0$ and the community
balance are handled in Steps 2--3.

\paragraph{Step 2: lifting to the $X$-space, $\feasbar_i$ and the binary diagonal.}
Introduce the lifted matrix $X_{ii}$ standing for $x_ix_i^{\top}$. Burer's
reformulation requires only the \emph{diagonal} of the squared linear system: squaring
each row $a_{i,j}^{\top}x_i=b_{i,j}$ of $A_ix_i=b_i$ and lifting gives, per row $j$, the
single scalar identity $a_{i,j}^{\top}X_{ii}a_{i,j}=b_{i,j}^{2}$. These define a set in
the $X$-space,
\begin{equation}
\feasbar_i:=\Big\{X_{ii}:\ \inner{a_{i,j}a_{i,j}^{\top}}{X_{ii}}=b_{i,j}^{2}\ \ \forall j\Big\}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{diag}\!\big(A_iX_{ii}A_i^{\top}\big)=b_i\circ b_i ,
\label{eq:Xbar}
\end{equation}
where $\inner{a a^{\top}}{X}=\Tr(aa^{\top}X)=a^{\top}Xa$ and $\circ$ is the entrywise
(Hadamard) product. Only the \emph{diagonal} entries $(A_iX_{ii}A_i^{\top})_{jj}=b_{i,j}^2$
are imposed --- the off-diagonal products are \emph{not} constrained, which is precisely
what Burer's theorem shows to be sufficient. The binary identity
$z_{i,m}-z_{i,m}^{2}=0$ lifts to the diagonal condition
\begin{equation}
Z_i=z_i,\qquad Z_{i,m}:=(X_{ii})_{mm}\ \ (m\in B_i),
\label{eq:bindiag}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the binary diagonal entries $Z_i$ of $X_{ii}$ equal the binary variables $z_i$
(since $z_{i,m}^{2}$ lifts to $(X_{ii})_{mm}$).

\paragraph{Step 3: the per-player lifted feasible set $\liftset_i$.}
Collect the per-player conditions of Steps 1--2 --- the $x$-space set $\feaseq_i$, the
$X$-space set $\feasbar_i$, and the binary diagonal \eqref{eq:bindiag} --- into a single
set of constraints on the pair $(x_i,X_{ii})$:
\begin{equation}
\liftset_i:=\big\{(x_i,X_{ii})\ :\ x_i\in\feaseq_i,\ \ X_{ii}\in\feasbar_i,\ \ Z_i=z_i\big\}.
\label{eq:Fi}
\end{equation}
This is purely affine (linear equalities and the diagonal binary identities); it carries
\emph{no} conic constraint, and the augmented block
$Y_i=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1 & x_i^{\top}\\ x_i & X_{ii}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ is
merely the matrix assembled from $(x_i,X_{ii})$. By construction $\liftset_i$ involves
only player $i$'s data. The only object not in $\liftset_i$ is the community balance,
which couples players and is kept separate.

\paragraph{The lift, preserving the block structure.}
\emph{Notational simplification for the lift.} To keep the lift readable we write the
chain below with the prosumer coupling alone --- a single family
$\sum_i\Acom_i\xcom_i=0$ and prosumer blocks $Y_i$ --- suppressing the common variables
$\xcommon$ and displaying the couplings as one aggregate row. Nothing in the lift depends
on this: the common variables add an extra polyhedral diagonal block $X_{00}$ lifted
identically (they carry no binaries), and the several homogeneous coupling rows (balance,
reserve, peak) are lifted together exactly as one, since their squared form is a single
aggregate identity $\Tr((\Acom)^{\top}\Acom X_S)=0$ over all rows (the zero right-hand
side makes each squared row nonnegative on $\CP$, so the aggregate vanishing forces each,
as in the footnote below). The general statement with $\xcommon$ and $\Azero$ is recorded
where it matters --- the cross-block vanishing (Lemma~\ref{lem:vanish},
eqs.~\eqref{eq:finiteness}--\eqref{eq:reduced-common}) and the core argument
(Lemma~\ref{lem:owen}). With that understood, assemble the joint matrix from the blocks,
\begin{equation}
Y_S=\begin{pmatrix}1 & x_S^{\top}\\ x_S & X_S\end{pmatrix},
\quad X_S=(X_{ij})_{i,j\in S},
\quad \inner{W_S}{Y_S}=\sum_{i\in S}w_i^{\top}x_i,
\label{eq:jointmatrix}
\end{equation}
with $x_S=(x_i)_{i\in S}$ and off-diagonal blocks $X_{ij}$ standing for
$x_ix_j^{\top}$. The lift proceeds by the chain
\begin{align}
\vmip(S)
&=\max_{\{x_i\}}\Big\{\textstyle\sum_i w_i^{\top}x_i:\
\underbrace{(x_i,X_{ii})\in\liftset_i\ \forall i}_{\text{per-player (affine)}},\
\underbrace{\textstyle\sum_i\Acom_i\xcom_i=0}_{\text{coupling}},\
\underbrace{X_S=x_Sx_S^{\top}}_{\text{rank one}}\Big\}
\label{eq:chain-rank1}\\[2pt]
&\le\max_{\{x_i\},X_S}\Big\{\textstyle\sum_i w_i^{\top}x_i:\
\underbrace{(x_i,X_{ii})\in\liftset_i\ \forall i}_{\text{per-player (affine)}},\
\underbrace{\textstyle\sum_i\Acom_i\xcom_i=0,\ \Tr\!\big((\Acom)^{\top}\Acom X_S\big)=0}_{\text{coupling, lifted}},\
\underbrace{Y_S\in\CP}_{\text{joint cone}}\Big\}
\label{eq:chain-cpp}\\[2pt]
&=\vmip(S).
\label{eq:chain-eq}
\end{align}

\noindent\emph{Line \eqref{eq:chain-rank1} --- exact rank-one reformulation.} Imposing
$X_S=x_Sx_S^{\top}$ (equivalently $X_{ij}=x_ix_j^{\top}$ for all $i,j$) reproduces every
product exactly; the affine per-player conditions $(x_i,X_{ii})\in\liftset_i$ then all
hold simultaneously, and the coupling is unchanged. This is a re-expression of
\eqref{eq:vmip-struct}, not a relaxation. (No conic constraint is needed here: rank-one
forces $Y_S$ to be a genuine outer product.)

\emph{Line \eqref{eq:chain-cpp} --- completely positive relaxation (upper bound).} The
rank-one constraint $X_S=x_Sx_S^{\top}$ is the sole nonconvexity. We drop it, requiring
instead the joint membership $Y_S\in\CP:=\mathrm{CPP}(\R_+^{1+\dim x_S})=\conv\{zz^{\top}:
z\ge0\}$ \citep[the dual of the copositive cone;][]{GuoEtAl}. To the coupling we adjoin
its \emph{square}
$\Tr\!\big((\Acom)^{\top}\Acom X_S\big)=0$.\footnote{The community balance has one row per
carrier $k$ and period $t$. Because the right-hand side is zero, the single aggregate
identity $\Tr((\Acom)^{\top}\Acom X_S)=0$
already forces \emph{each} row's squared balance to vanish: on $Y_S\in\CP$ every summand
$\inner{(\Acom_{kt})^{\top}\Acom_{kt}}{X_S}$ is nonnegative (a quadratic form evaluated on a
completely positive matrix), so a zero sum forces each term to zero. One scalar
constraint thus encodes all rows.} The block structure is preserved: every
constraint in $\liftset_i$ touches only the diagonal block $X_{ii}$, while the
\emph{squared community balance} is the one term reaching the off-diagonal blocks
$X_{ij}$. Since a CP matrix is a convex combination of rank-one terms,
$X_S\neq x_Sx_S^{\top}$ in general; as we maximize, enlarging the feasible set gives
``$\le$''.

\emph{Line \eqref{eq:chain-eq} --- equality (Burer's theorem).} Under the key assumption
of Theorem~\ref{thm:burer}, verified in Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}, the relaxation is
exact and ``$\le$'' becomes ``$=$''. We abbreviate the resulting convex program as
\begin{equation}
\vmip(S)=\max_{Y_S}\ \Big\{\inner{W_S}{Y_S}:\
\underbrace{(x_i,X_{ii})\in\liftset_i\ \forall i\in S}_{\text{(per-player, affine)}},\
\underbrace{\textstyle\sum_i\Acom_i\xcom_i=0}_{\text{(c)}},\
\underbrace{\Tr\!\big((\Acom)^{\top}\Acom X_S\big)=0}_{\text{(d)}},\
\underbrace{Y_S\in\CP}_{\text{(e)}}\Big\}=:\mathrm{CPP}(S).
\label{eq:cpp}
\end{equation}
The per-player sets $\liftset_i$ are intra-block; the coupling enters only through the
linear balance (c) and its square (d), the latter being the sole term on the
off-diagonal blocks $X_{ij}$. In particular the prosumer balance, being part of
$\feaseq_i$, is lifted entirely inside $X_{ii}$ and creates no cross-player term ---
which is why ``partial lift'' difficulties do not arise here.

\paragraph{Exactness via Burer's reformulation (Bomze \& Gabl, Thm.~4).}
The equality \eqref{eq:chain-eq} is an instance of Burer's completely positive
reformulation. We quote it in the form of Bomze and Gabl and verify its hypothesis.
The set-completely positive cone over a closed convex cone $K\subseteq\R^{N}$ is
$\mathrm{CPP}(K):=\conv\{xx^{\top}:x\in K\}$ \citep[][\S2.1]{BomzeGabl2023}.

\begin{theorem}[{Burer's reformulation; \citealp[Thm.~4]{BomzeGabl2023}, after
\citealp{Burer2009}}]
\label{thm:burer}
Let $K\subseteq\R^{N}$ be a closed convex cone and
$L:=\{x\in K:Ax=b\}\neq\varnothing$, with recession cone $L_{\infty}=\{x\in K:Ax=o\}$.
Let $Q_i\in\mathcal{S}^{N}$, $q_i\in\R^{N}$, $i\in[1{:}l]$, and
$B:=\{j:Q_ie_j=o\text{ or }q_i^{\top}e_j=0\text{ for some }i\}$. Assume the
\emph{key assumption}: \textup{(a)} $x^{\top}Q_ix+2q_i^{\top}x\ge\omega_i$ for all
$x\in L$, $i\in[1{:}l]$; \textup{(b)} $d\in L_{\infty}\Rightarrow d_j=0$ for all $j\in B$.
Then the QCQP
$\min_{x\in K}\{x^{\top}Q_0x+2q_0^{\top}x: Ax=b,\ x^{\top}Q_ix+q_i^{\top}x=\omega_i\}$
equals the completely positive program
\[
\min_{x,X}\ Q_0\bullet X+q_0^{\top}x
\ \ \text{s.t.}\ \
Ax=b,\ \operatorname{Diag}(AXA^{\top})=b\circ b,\
Q_i\bullet X+q_i^{\top}x=\omega_i,\
\begin{bmatrix}1 & x^{\top}\\ x & X\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{CPP}(\R_+\times K).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[\eqref{eq:cpp} is exact]
\label{prop:exact}
The coalition program \eqref{eq:vmip-struct}, with each $\feas_i$ put in equality form
$\feaseq_i$ \eqref{eq:Xeq}, satisfies the key assumption of Theorem~\ref{thm:burer};
hence \eqref{eq:cpp} holds with equality.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Pass to the minimization $-\vmip(S)=\min\{-\sum_i w_i^{\top}x_i:\dots\}$ to match the
theorem's convention. Stack the (slacked) variables $x=x_S\ge0$ and take
$K=\R_+^{\dim x_S}$. The linear system $Ax=b$ collects \emph{all} linear equalities:
the per-player blocks $A_ix_i=b_i$ defining $\feaseq_i$ (including the slack identities
$z+\sigma=1$) and the community balance $\sum_i\Acom_i\xcom_i=0$. The only quadratic
equalities are the binary identities $z_{i,m}-z_{i,m}^{2}=0$ ($m\in B_i$), i.e.\
$Q=-e_me_m^{\top}$, $q=e_m$, $\omega=0$ for each. The slack reformulation
\eqref{eq:binstd} guarantees $z_{i,m}\in[0,1]$ on $L$. As Bomze and Gabl note, the
identity $z_{i,m}-z_{i,m}^{2}=0$ then meets the key assumption: \textup{(a)} holds since
$z_{i,m}-z_{i,m}^{2}\ge0$ on $[0,1]$, and \textup{(b)} holds since each $\feas_i$ is
bounded, so $L$ is bounded, $L_{\infty}=\{o\}$, and the condition on $B$ is vacuous
\citep[][following Thm.~4]{BomzeGabl2023}. Theorem~\ref{thm:burer} applies, and its CPP
is exactly the program in line \eqref{eq:chain-cpp}: the requirement
$\operatorname{Diag}(AXA^{\top})=b\circ b$ is the squared form of the full system
$Ax=b$, which decomposes into the per-player diagonal RLT identities
$\operatorname{diag}(A_iX_{ii}A_i^{\top})=b_i\circ b_i$ defining $\feasbar_i$ (from
$A_ix_i=b_i$) and the squared community balance $\Tr((\Acom)^{\top}\Acom X_S)=0$ (from
$\sum_i\Acom_i\xcom_i=0$), while the diagonal conditions $Z_i=z_i$ \eqref{eq:bindiag} are
the lifted binary identities. Hence \eqref{eq:cpp} is an equality.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The objective matrix $W_S$.}
The original objective $\sum_{i\in S}w_i^{\top}x_i$ is \emph{linear} in the variables, so
in the lifted space it touches only the first row/column of $Y_S$ (the row carrying
$x_i$), never the product blocks $X_{ij}$. Concretely, per player,
\begin{equation}
W_i=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0 & w_i^{\top}\\ w_i & 0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad\text{so that}\qquad
\inner{W_i}{Y_i}=\Tr(W_iY_i)=w_i^{\top}x_i,
\label{eq:Wmat}
\end{equation}
and $W_S$ is the block-diagonal assembly $W_S=\operatorname{diag}(W_i)_{i\in S}$ --- it has
\emph{no} off-diagonal ($X_{ij}$) blocks, because the objective contains no inter-player
product. Thus $\inner{W_S}{Y_S}=\sum_{i\in S}w_i^{\top}x_i$ recovers \eqref{eq:vmip}.

\paragraph{Block notation.}
Diagonal blocks $X_{ii}$ are player-internal; off-diagonal blocks $X_{ij}$ ($i\neq j$)
are inter-player. Each player block, with its augmenting row, is
$Y_i=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1 & x_i^{\top}\\ x_i & X_{ii}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$.

\paragraph{What dualizing the joint cone leaves behind: a preview.}
The construction dualizes the joint cone $Y_S\in\CP$ (and the squared coupling). The
point to keep in view is what survives in the inner problem afterward. \emph{Before}
dualization the joint cone is the only constraint reaching the off-diagonal blocks
$X_{ij}$; the diagonal blocks $X_{ii}$ are already pinned by the affine set $\liftset_i$
(through the RLT identities $\feasbar_i$, which are the squares of the variable bounds).
\emph{After} dualization the joint cone is moved into the objective and no longer
constrains the matrix, so the only constraints left are the per-player \emph{affine}
sets $\liftset_i$ and the linear coupling. We do \emph{not} re-impose a per-player cone
$Y_i\in\CP_i$: it is unnecessary. The off-diagonal blocks vanish from the value by the
coefficient argument of Section~\ref{sec:vanish} (their linear coefficient is forced to
zero), and the diagonal blocks $X_{ii}$ remain bounded by $\liftset_i$ alone. What
results is therefore a \emph{classical} linear production game over the affine resource
sets $\liftset_i$, not a conic one.

\begin{remark}[On the per-player cone $Y_i\in\CP_i$: redundant in the primal, and not
needed in the dual either]
\label{rem:no-perplayer-cone}
A principal submatrix of a completely positive matrix is completely positive, so
$Y_S\in\CP\Rightarrow Y_i\in\CP_i$ for each $i$; hence the per-player cone is
\emph{redundant} in the primal $\mathrm{CPP}(N)$ \eqref{eq:cpp} and need not be written.
One might worry that, once the joint cone is dualized and this implication is lost, a
per-player cone must be re-imposed to keep the inner problem bounded. It need not. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:vanish} the off-diagonal blocks $X_{ij}$ drop out of the value (their
coefficient is driven to zero), and the diagonal blocks $X_{ii}$ are bounded by the RLT
identities already inside $\liftset_i$. The inner problem is therefore bounded over the
affine sets $\liftset_i$ alone, and adding $Y_i\in\CP_i$ would only re-constrain a matrix
that is already pinned where it matters --- changing the inner program to a \emph{different}
(more constrained) one whose Lagrangian dual need not equal $\vmip(N)$. We accordingly
keep the inner sets affine: the dualization target is the joint cone (e) and the squared
coupling (d), nothing more.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Two distinct gaps --- read this first.}
The note turns on keeping two unrelated notions of ``gap'' separate; conflating
them produces an apparent contradiction.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Gap \arabic*.},leftmargin=3.2em]
\item \textbf{Duality gap} --- between the primal CPP \eqref{eq:cpp} and its Lagrangian
dual \eqref{eq:dual}. Because the CPP is \emph{convex}, this gap is \textbf{zero} under
Slater (conic strong duality); it is \emph{always} closed here:
\begin{align}
\text{CPP}\ \longleftrightarrow\ \min_{M,\lambda} \vlr_N(M,\lambda),
\qquad
\min_{M,\lambda}\vlr_N(M,\lambda)=\text{CPP}=\vmip(N).
\label{eq:gap1}
\end{align}
\item \textbf{Integrality gap} --- between the mixed-integer value and its convex
relaxation:
\begin{align}
\vmip(N)\ \longleftrightarrow\ \vchp(N),
\qquad \vchp(N)-\vmip(N)\ \ge\ 0.
\label{eq:gap2}
\end{align}
This has \emph{nothing to do with duality} and is \textbf{not} generally zero; it
measures the non-convexity of $\prod_i \feas_i$ under the coupling, a property of the model
instance.
\end{enumerate}
The crucial point is that \textbf{Gap~1 being zero does \emph{not} imply Gap~2 is
zero.} Strong duality says $\min_M \vlr_N=\vmip$, and $\vchp$ does not appear in
\eqref{eq:gap1}. Where $\vchp$ \emph{does} appear is at a \emph{fixed} (non-optimal)
multiplier: by Section~\ref{sec:vanish} a fixed $M$ makes the cross blocks drop out,
leaving a per-player convex-hull problem. Collecting the relationships,
\begin{align}
\vmip(N)
&=\underbrace{\min_{M,\lambda} \vlr_N(M,\lambda)}_{\text{Gap 1 closed: optimal }M}
\ \le\
\underbrace{\vlr_N(M,\lambda)}_{\text{generic fixed }M:\ \text{convex-hull level}},
\label{eq:walkdown}\\[2pt]
\underbrace{\vchp(N)-\vmip(N)}_{\text{Gap 2}}
&=\text{the slack between the two sides of \eqref{eq:walkdown}.}
\notag
\end{align}
So the optimal multiplier $M^{*}$ attains $\vmip(N)$; a generic fixed multiplier sits at
the convex-hull level; whether they coincide (so that any multiplier suffices) is
precisely Gap~2. \textbf{The LRB uses the optimal multiplier}, so the total it
distributes is $\vlr_N(M^{*})=\vmip(N)$ --- budget-balanced at $\vmip$, and $\vchp$ is only
a way-station the dual minimization passes through, never the budget of the final
allocation (Remark~\ref{rem:Mcone}). Read everything below with this distinction in
hand. The companion construction of Section~\ref{sec:chp} makes the way-station explicit:
it is exactly the program one obtains by \emph{not} dualizing --- lifting only the
per-player sets and keeping the linear coupling --- and it equals convex hull pricing.

\section{The construction}
\label{sec:construction}

\paragraph{The Lagrangian-relaxation bound (LRB) framework.}
Our construction follows the Lagrangian-relaxation framework of \citet{LiuEtAl} for
computing stable allocations of mixed-integer cooperative games. In their
(cost-minimization) setting, one relaxes a chosen family of coupling constraints into the
objective with a multiplier $\lambda\ge0$, obtaining a relaxed characteristic function
$c^{\mathrm{LR}}(\cdot;\lambda)$ that is a coalition-wise \emph{lower} bound on the true
cost $c(\cdot)$. The core mechanism by which they recover a stable allocation is that the
restriction of the grand-coalition Lagrangian dual solution to a sub-coalition is
feasible for that coalition's relaxed problem, so weak duality makes the dual-based
allocation stable.

In Liu's IM-game formulation the relaxed coupling has the form $A'x\ge B'\gamma_s+D'$,
where $\gamma_s$ is a \emph{coalition indicator} appearing on the right-hand side. This
is what lets them split $c^{\mathrm{LR}}(\cdot;\lambda)=c^{\mathrm{LR}_1}(\cdot;\lambda)+
c^{\mathrm{LR}_2}(\cdot;\lambda)$ \citep[][eqs.~(6)--(7)]{LiuEtAl} into a closed-form
sub-game~1 carrying the $\gamma_s$-linear term $\lambda B'\gamma_s$ and a residual
sub-game~2. \emph{Our coupling has no such structure.} The community balance
$\sum_i\Acom_i\xcom_i=0$ is homogeneous --- there is no coalition-indicator right-hand
side --- so the term that would constitute Liu's sub-game~1 is absent, and the
decomposition collapses: there is a single relaxed game, not two. We therefore do
\emph{not} split into sub-games; we work directly with the relaxed Lagrangian and show it
is a linear production game.

We transpose the framework to our profit-maximization setting. The role of the relaxed
lower bound is played by the Lagrangian of the copositive program $\mathrm{CPP}(S)$
\eqref{eq:cpp}, which (in the maximization convention) is a coalition-wise \emph{upper}
bound on $\vmip$. The question is \emph{which} constraints to dualize. The community
program has exactly one inter-player coupling --- the community balance --- lifted into
the linear balance (c) and its square (d), plus the joint cone (e) that ties the
off-diagonal blocks together. We dualize the two constraints that carry the
\emph{inter-block} coupling at the lifted level: the joint cone (e) and the squared
balance (d). Everything else --- the per-player affine sets $\liftset_i$ and the linear
balance (c) --- is kept explicit. As Section~\ref{sec:vanish} shows, this choice makes
the off-diagonal blocks drop out of the value, leaving a relaxed Lagrangian that is
block-separable: a (classical) linear production game, solvable by a single Owen
allocation rather than by column generation. Sections~\ref{sec:vanish}--\ref{sec:composition}
carry this out.

\paragraph{Step A (dualize the joint cone and the squared coupling).}
Starting from $\mathrm{CPP}(S)$ \eqref{eq:cpp}, we dualize exactly two constraint
families and keep the rest explicit:
\begin{align}
\text{dualize:}\quad
&\text{(e) joint cone } Y_S\in\CP
&&\rightsquigarrow\ \text{matrix multiplier } M\in\COP\ \text{(dual cone)},\notag\\
&\text{(d) squared coupling } \Tr\!\big((\Acom)^{\top}\Acom X_S\big)=0
&&\rightsquigarrow\ \text{scalar multiplier } \lambda\in\R;\notag\\
\text{keep explicit:}\quad
&(x_i,X_{ii})\in\liftset_i\ (\forall i),
&&\text{i.e.\ the per-player \emph{affine} sets;}\notag\\
&\text{the linear coupling (c) } \textstyle\sum_i\Acom_i\xcom_i=0. &&
\label{eq:dualize-keep}
\end{align}
The resulting Lagrangian characteristic function --- the profit analogue of Liu's
$c^{\mathrm{LR}}(\cdot;\lambda)$ \citep[][eq.~(4)]{LiuEtAl} --- is
\begin{align}
\vlr_S(M,\lambda)=\max_{\substack{(x_i,X_{ii})\in\liftset_i\ \forall i\in S\\[1pt]
\sum_{i\in S}\Acom_i\xcom_i=0}}
\ \inner{W_S}{Y_S}+\inner{M}{Y_S}
-\lambda\,\Tr\!\big((\Acom)^{\top}\Acom X_S\big),
\label{eq:lagrangian}
\end{align}
in which the off-diagonal blocks $X_{ij}$, $i\neq j$, are now \emph{free} --- no cone
constrains them, the joint cone having been moved to the objective. The Lagrangian dual
is
\begin{align}
\dual^{*}(S)=\min_{M\in\COP,\ \lambda}\ \vlr_S(M,\lambda).
\label{eq:dual}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Step B (solve the dual, recover a stable allocation).}
We solve \eqref{eq:dual} for the grand coalition, obtaining a multiplier
$(M^{*},\lambda^{*})$, and from the resulting LPG we read off an allocation, which
we claim is in the core of $\Gamma^{\mathrm{MIP}}$. The rest of the note justifies this.

\section{What survives in the value: the cross blocks vanish}
\label{sec:vanish}

\paragraph{Why the off-diagonal blocks are free, while the diagonal blocks are not.}
Before computing coefficients we must justify treating the off-diagonal blocks $X_{ij}$
($i\neq j$) as \emph{free} (hence able to send the maximization to $+\infty$), since the
whole argument hinges on it. The point is an asymmetry between diagonal and off-diagonal
blocks.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{Diagonal blocks $X_{ii}$ are bounded.} They are constrained by
$\liftset_i$, in particular by the RLT identities in $\feasbar_i$
\eqref{eq:Xbar}, $\operatorname{diag}(A_iX_{ii}A_i^{\top})=b_i\circ b_i$. These are
precisely the squares of the variable bounds: e.g.\ squaring the slacked bound
$z+\sigma=1$ and lifting gives
$(X_{ii})_{zz}+2(X_{ii})_{z\sigma}+(X_{ii})_{\sigma\sigma}=1$, which confines the
relevant entries of $X_{ii}$ to a bounded range. So the bounds we introduced in the
equality reformulation \eqref{eq:binstd} \emph{do} survive in $\liftset_i$ --- bounding
$X_{ii}$.
\item \emph{Off-diagonal blocks $X_{ij}$ are not bounded.} The block $X_{ij}$ belongs to
\emph{no} per-player set: it is in neither $\liftset_i$ nor $\liftset_j$ (each
contains only its own $x_\bullet,X_{\bullet\bullet}$), so no RLT identity touches it. Nor
is it tied to $x_ix_j^{\top}$: that link held only under the rank-one constraint, which
was dropped in passing to \eqref{eq:chain-cpp}. Thus $X_{ij}$ is decoupled from the
(bounded) variables $x_i,x_j$ and ranges over the full symmetric block. Boundedness of
$x_i,x_j$ does \emph{not} bound the independent matrix variable $X_{ij}$.
\end{itemize}
The only constraint that ever reached $X_{ij}$ was the joint cone $Y_S\in\CP$, and that
was moved to the objective in Step~A. Hence $X_{ij}$ is genuinely free, and a nonzero
linear coefficient on it makes $\vlr_S=+\infty$. This is what powers the coefficient
argument below; the same argument does \emph{not} apply to $X_{ii}$, which is bounded and
is instead absorbed into the modified objective $\widetilde W_i$. (This asymmetry is also
why no per-player cone is needed: the would-be-unbounded blocks $X_{ij}$ are tamed by the
coefficient condition, not by a cone, and the bounded blocks $X_{ii}$ need no cone;
cf.\ Remark~\ref{rem:no-perplayer-cone}.)

The off-diagonal blocks $X_{ij}$ enter \eqref{eq:lagrangian} linearly, and we now
determine their coefficients explicitly. The squared coupling involves only the
community-flow entries (since $\Acom$ acts on $\xcom$), whereas the dualized cone term
$\inner{M}{Y_S}$ touches all entries. Writing the cross block $X_{ij}$ in its
$(\mathrm{c},\mathrm{r})\times(\mathrm{c},\mathrm{r})$ sub-entries (c\,$=$\,com,
r\,$=$\,rest) and using the symmetry $X_{ji}=X_{ij}^{\top}$, the coefficient of each free
cross-entry is as follows.

\begin{lemma}[Finiteness conditions and vanishing of the cross blocks]
\label{lem:vanish}
$\vlr_S(M,\lambda)$ is finite only if, for every $i\neq j$, the cross block $M_{ij}$
satisfies
\begin{equation}
(M_{ij})_{cc}=\lambda\,(\Acom_i)^{\top}\Acom_j,\qquad
(M_{ij})_{cr}=(M_{ij})_{rc}=(M_{ij})_{rr}=0 .
\label{eq:finiteness}
\end{equation}
Here $(M_{ij})_{cc}=\lambda(\Acom_i)^{\top}\Acom_j$ is a \emph{matrix} identity of size
$n^{\mathrm{com}}_i\times n^{\mathrm{com}}_j$: with $\Acom_i\in\R^{R\times n^{\mathrm{com}}_i}$
($R=|K||T|$ balance rows), $(\Acom_i)^{\top}\Acom_j=\sum_{r=1}^{R}(\Acom_i)_{r,:}^{\top}
(\Acom_j)_{r,:}$ is summed over all carrier--period balance rows (it reduces to the
scalar $a_ia_j$ only when $n^{\mathrm{com}}=R=1$). The symmetry factor of $2$ arising
from $X_{ji}=X_{ij}^{\top}$ appears on both sides and cancels.
Under \eqref{eq:finiteness}, every free cross-entry has zero coefficient, so the cross
blocks contribute nothing to the value, and
\begin{equation}
\vlr_S(M,\lambda)=\max_{\substack{(x_i,X_{ii})\in\liftset_i\ \forall i\in S\\ \sum_{i\in S}\Acom_i\xcom_i=0}}
\ \sum_{i\in S}\inner{\widetilde W_i}{Y_i},
\qquad
\widetilde W_i:=W_i+M_{ii}-\lambda\,(\Acom_i)^{\top}\Acom_i\,\Pi_{cc},
\label{eq:reduced}
\end{equation}
where $(\Acom_i)^{\top}\Acom_i$ ($n^{\mathrm{com}}_i\times n^{\mathrm{com}}_i$, summed
over balance rows) sits on the $(\mathrm{c},\mathrm{c})$ sub-block via $\Pi_{cc}$, and
$\widetilde W_i$ depends only on player $i$'s own data.

The same holds for the \emph{prosumer--common} cross blocks $X_{i0}$ ($i\in\N$), which
arise once the common variables $\xcommon$ are lifted alongside the prosumers (this is
matrix bookkeeping in the lift, not the introduction of a player): finiteness forces
\begin{equation}
(M_{i0})_{cc}=\lambda\,(\Acom_i)^{\top}\Azero,\qquad
(M_{i0})_{cr}=(M_{i0})_{rc}=(M_{i0})_{rr}=0,
\label{eq:finiteness-common}
\end{equation}
so $X_{i0}$ contributes nothing free to the value either, and the reduced value
\eqref{eq:reduced} acquires one further summand for the common-variable block,
\begin{equation}
\vlr_S(M,\lambda)=\max_{\substack{(x_i,X_{ii})\in\liftset_i\ \forall i\in S\\[1pt]
(\xcommon,X_{00})\in\liftset_0,\ \ \sum_{i\in S}\Acom_i\xcom_i+\Azero\xcommon=0}}
\ \sum_{i\in S}\inner{\widetilde W_i}{Y_i}\ +\ \inner{\widetilde W_0}{Y_0},
\qquad
\widetilde W_0:=W_0+M_{00}-\lambda\,\Azero^{\top}\Azero\,\Pi_{cc},
\label{eq:reduced-common}
\end{equation}
where $Y_0=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\xcommon^{\top}\\\xcommon&X_{00}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
is the lifted common-variable block and
$\liftset_0=\{(\xcommon,X_{00}):\xcommon\ge0,\ \text{RLT identities of the common
variables}\}$ its (polyhedral) feasible description. The block $Y_0$ is a shared column
group, present in every coalition's program and chosen by the coalition; it is not a
player and receives no allocation (Lemma~\ref{lem:owen}, Remark~\ref{rem:common-tech}).
Thus the cross blocks reaching $\xcommon$ vanish exactly as the prosumer--prosumer ones
do, and the common variables enter the value only through their own diagonal block.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The blocks $X_{ij}$ and $X_{ji}=X_{ij}^{\top}$ both occur in the joint matrix $Y_S$
(in the $(i,j)$ and $(j,i)$ positions), and the dual $M$ likewise has $M_{ij}$ and
$M_{ji}=M_{ij}^{\top}$. Using $\inner{A}{B}=\inner{A^{\top}}{B^{\top}}$, the two
positions contribute equally, so every off-diagonal coefficient carries a common factor
$2$. The coefficient of the free entry $(X_{ij})_{cc}$ is therefore
$2\bigl[(M_{ij})_{cc}-\lambda(\Acom_i)^{\top}\Acom_j\bigr]$: the first term from
$\inner{M}{Y_S}$, the second from $-\lambda\Tr((\Acom)^\top \Acom X_S)$, whose off-diagonal
com--com coefficient is $a_i a_j$ in the scalar case and $(\Acom_i)^\top \Acom_j$ in
general. The coefficient of $(X_{ij})_{cr}$, $(X_{ij})_{rc}$, $(X_{ij})_{rr}$ is
$2(M_{ij})_{cr}$, $2(M_{ij})_{rc}$, $2(M_{ij})_{rr}$ respectively, since the squared
coupling has no rest-component. A free variable with nonzero linear coefficient makes the
maximization $+\infty$; hence finiteness forces each coefficient to zero. The common
factor $2$ divides out, leaving \eqref{eq:finiteness}. Substituting, the only surviving
part of the dualized terms is the diagonal contribution
$\sum_{i\in S}\bigl[\inner{M_{ii}}{Y_i}-\lambda(\Acom_i)^\top \Acom_i (X_{ii})_{cc}\bigr]$,
giving \eqref{eq:reduced}. Each summand involves only block $i$.
The prosumer--common cross blocks $X_{i0}$ are handled identically: $X_{i0}$ and
$X_{0i}=X_{i0}^{\top}$ occur symmetrically, their $(\mathrm{c},\mathrm{c})$ coefficient is
$2[(M_{i0})_{cc}-\lambda(\Acom_i)^{\top}\Azero]$ and the other sub-blocks
$2(M_{i0})_{cr},2(M_{i0})_{rc},2(M_{i0})_{rr}$, all forced to zero by finiteness, giving
\eqref{eq:finiteness-common}; the surviving common-block diagonal term
$\inner{M_{00}}{Y_0}-\lambda\Azero^{\top}\Azero(X_{00})_{cc}$ yields the extra summand of
\eqref{eq:reduced-common}. (The common variables carry no binaries, so their lift is the
plain polyhedral RLT block.)
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Two different mechanisms, one effect]
\label{rem:twomech}
It is worth stating precisely why the cross blocks vanish, since this was a point of
confusion. The $(\mathrm{c},\mathrm{c})$ cross-entry vanishes by \emph{cancellation}: the squared-coupling
term $\lambda(\Acom_i)^\top \Acom_j$ is exactly matched by the dual cross-block
$(M_{ij})_{cc}$. The $(\mathrm{c},\mathrm{r})$, $(\mathrm{r},\mathrm{c})$, $(\mathrm{r},\mathrm{r})$ cross-entries vanish by \emph{forcing}:
the squared coupling contributes nothing there, so the dual cross-block is driven to
zero. Both are consequences of finiteness, and finiteness is not an external assumption
but a property of the dual feasible set: since \eqref{eq:dual} is a minimization, any
$M$ giving $\vlr_S=+\infty$ is never selected, so the dual automatically restricts to
multipliers satisfying \eqref{eq:finiteness}. The squared coupling was dualized
\emph{precisely} so that the $(\mathrm{c},\mathrm{c})$ cross-entry could cancel rather than be forced to
zero; this is what keeps the inter-player coupling information present (through
$\lambda$) instead of discarding it.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The cross blocks are \emph{eliminated}, not merely zero-valued; in what
sense the Lagrangian ``is'' an LPG]
\label{rem:eliminate}
A careful reader will note that the off-diagonal blocks $X_{ij}$ ($i\neq j$) are still
formally present as \emph{variables} in the Lagrangian \eqref{eq:lagrangian}: the inner
maximization is over the full matrix $Y_S$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:vanish} does not delete
$X_{ij}$ --- it shows that the dual forces their objective coefficient to zero. We make
the passage from this to a linear production game precise, since the two are not
literally the same object.

At any dual-feasible $(M,\lambda)$ satisfying \eqref{eq:finiteness}, each $X_{ij}$
($i\neq j$) (i) appears in \emph{no} constraint --- it lies in no per-player set
$\liftset_i$ and not in the linear coupling --- and (ii) has zero coefficient in the
objective. A variable that is both unconstrained and absent from the objective can be
removed without changing the optimal value or the optimal $(x_i,X_{ii})$: its value is
\emph{indeterminate} (any assignment is optimal and feasible), so fixing it arbitrarily,
say $X_{ij}=0$, and deleting it leaves an equivalent program. Carrying out this
elimination turns \eqref{eq:lagrangian} into the \emph{reduced} program
\eqref{eq:subgame2def}, in which only the per-player variables $(x_i,X_{ii})$ remain.
The two programs share the same optimal value; they differ only in that the reduced one
has discarded variables that could not affect it.

This is the precise content of ``the relaxed Lagrangian is an LPG.'' It is the
\emph{reduced} program \eqref{eq:subgame2def}, not the raw Lagrangian
\eqref{eq:lagrangian}, that is a linear production game: only after eliminating $X_{ij}$
is every remaining variable attributable to a single player, as Owen's framework
requires. The elimination is what makes the attribution well-defined; a cross block
$X_{ij}$ belongs to neither player $i$ nor player $j$, so a formulation retaining it
would not be a production game. Two consequences worth recording:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{No ``LPG\,$+$\,residual'' split.} One might hope to peel $X_{ij}$ off into a
separate sub-problem and solve an LPG plus a remainder. There is nothing to peel: $X_{ij}$
sits in no constraint, so it forms no sub-problem with independent content (with zero
coefficient its ``sub-problem'' is the trivial $\max 0$). The only correct operation is
elimination, not separation. (This is unrelated to Liu's two-sub-game split, which our
homogeneous coupling already precludes for a different reason, Section~\ref{sec:composition}.)
\item \emph{Indeterminacy of $X_{ij}$ does not blur the allocation.} Because the optimal
$X_{ij}$ is indeterminate, the dual solution does not pin it --- but this is harmless. The
Owen allocation \eqref{eq:owen} is computed from $g_i,b_i$ and the duals
$\nu_i^{*},\mu^{*}$ of the \emph{reduced} program \eqref{eq:subgame2def}, none of which
involves $X_{ij}$. The cross blocks' indeterminacy therefore leaves the allocation
uniquely determined by the reduced LPG's data.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\section{The relaxed Lagrangian is a linear production game with a nonempty core}
\label{sec:coniclpg}

\paragraph{The relaxed game.}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:vanish}, at any dual-feasible $(M,\lambda)$ the reduced Lagrangian
\eqref{eq:reduced} --- obtained from \eqref{eq:lagrangian} by eliminating the
zero-coefficient, unconstrained cross blocks $X_{ij}$, which is value-preserving
(Remark~\ref{rem:eliminate}) --- is the characteristic function of a single (profit)
cooperative game $(N,\vlr(\cdot;M,\lambda))$, with
\begin{equation}
\vlr_S(M,\lambda)=\
\begin{aligned}[t]
\max_{\{(x_i,X_{ii})\},\,(\xcommon,X_{00})}\quad & \sum_{i\in S}\inner{\widetilde W_i}{Y_i}\ +\ \inner{\widetilde W_0}{Y_0}\footnotemark\\[2pt]
\text{s.t.}\quad & \sum_{i\in S}\Acom_i\xcom_i\ +\ \Azero\xcommon\ =\ 0,\\[2pt]
& (x_i,X_{ii})\in\liftset_i\quad\forall i\in S,\qquad (\xcommon,X_{00})\in\liftset_0,
\end{aligned}
\qquad S\subseteq N,
\label{eq:subgame2def}
\end{equation}
\footnotetext{The decision variables are the pair $(x_i,X_{ii})$; the matrix
$Y_i=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1 & x_i^{\top}\\ x_i & X_{ii}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ is
merely their assembly, with the $(1,1)$ entry fixed at $1$. Thus
$\inner{\widetilde W_i}{Y_i}=\Tr(\widetilde W_iY_i)$ is an \emph{affine} function of
$(x_i,X_{ii})$: the first row/column of $\widetilde W_i$ contributes the linear term in
$x_i$, the lower-right block contributes the linear term in $X_{ii}$, and the
$(1,1)$ entry contributes only an additive constant (irrelevant to the optimization).
Writing the objective in $Y_i$ and the constraints in $(x_i,X_{ii})$ is therefore the
same program; the $Y_i$-form is used only to keep the inner products compact.}
the maximization analogue of Liu's relaxed characteristic function
$c^{\mathrm{LR}}(\cdot;\lambda)$ \citep[][eq.~(4)]{LiuEtAl}, here already reduced to its
block-separable form by Lemma~\ref{lem:vanish}. A profit allocation $\alpha\in\R^{N}$ is
\emph{stable} for this game if $\sum_{i\in S}\alpha_i\ge \vlr_S(M,\lambda)$ for all
$S\subseteq N$, and lies in the \emph{core} if in addition
$\sum_{i\in N}\alpha_i=\vlr_N(M,\lambda)$. We show this game has a nonempty core by
exhibiting a structural Owen allocation.

\begin{remark}[The multiplier is fixed at the grand-coalition value; the objective
coefficients are coalition-invariant]
\label{rem:fixed-multiplier}
A subtlety in reading \eqref{eq:subgame2def} as a production game deserves to be made
explicit, since the objective coefficient
$\widetilde W_i=W_i+M_{ii}-\lambda(\Acom_i)^{\top}\Acom_i\Pi_{cc}$ depends on the dual
variables $(M,\lambda)$, which might appear to vary with the coalition. It does not. By
construction, $\vlr_S(\cdot)$ is the Lagrangian sub-game of the \emph{grand-coalition}
copositive program at a \emph{single, fixed} multiplier $(M,\lambda)$: the multiplier is
an exogenous parameter of the game, not optimized per coalition, and is the same for
every $S\subseteq N$ (note $(M,\lambda)$ carries no $S$-dependence in
\eqref{eq:subgame2def}). For the existence theorem we fix it at the grand-coalition dual
optimum $(M^{*},\lambda^{*})$. Consequently the coefficient $\widetilde W_i$ is a
\emph{constant} of player $i$ once $(M^{*},\lambda^{*})$ is fixed, identical across all
coalitions containing $i$. This is exactly the data Owen's theorem requires --- a fixed,
coalition-invariant linear objective --- and it is what makes the Owen restriction of
Lemma~\ref{lem:owen} (restricting the grand-coalition dual solution to a sub-coalition)
legitimate: the sub-coalition game $\vlr_S$ is not a re-optimized object but the same
fixed-coefficient program read on the blocks $i\in S$. Likewise the cross-block
finiteness condition \eqref{eq:finiteness}, imposed for all $i\neq j$ in $N$, holds
\emph{a fortiori} for any pair inside $S\subseteq N$, so the cross blocks vanish in
$\vlr_S$ too and each $\vlr_S$ is genuinely the (classical) LPG below. In short: one
multiplier, fixed at $N$; the coalition structure lives entirely in which blocks and
which copies of the linear coupling are present, never in the objective coefficients.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{The relaxed game is a linear production game with common technology.}
The prosumer resource sets $\liftset_i$ are \emph{affine} (linear equalities and the
diagonal binary identities, together with $x_i\ge0$ inherited from $\feaseq_i$), and the
common-variable set $\liftset_0=\{\xcommon\ge0,\ \text{RLT of the common variables}\}$ is
polyhedral; there is no conic constraint left after Step~A. The objective
$\sum_{i\in S}\inner{\widetilde W_i}{Y_i}+\inner{\widetilde W_0}{Y_0}$ is linear and the
only coupling is the homogeneous balance
$\sum_{i\in S}\Acom_i\xcom_i+\Azero\xcommon=0$. This is exactly the data of a linear
production game \emph{with common technology} --- linear objective, polyhedral resource
sets, one shared homogeneous coupling, and a block of shared decision variables $\xcommon$
controlled by each coalition --- in the sense of \citet{Owen1975} as generalized by
\citet{Uhan2015}. The players are the prosumers $\N$; $\xcommon$ is not a player. We invoke
this classical result directly, with no conic generalization.

\begin{lemma}[LPG with common technology has a nonempty core]
\label{lem:owen}
Consider the grand-coalition program \eqref{eq:subgame2def} ($S=N$), a linear program in
the prosumer variables $\{(x_i,X_{ii})\}_{i\in N}$ \emph{and} the common variables
$(\xcommon,X_{00})$, with a single homogeneous coupling
$\sum_{i\in N}T_iY_i+T_0Y_0=0$ (here $T_iY_i=\Acom_i\xcom_i$, $T_0Y_0=\Azero\xcommon$).
For a coalition $S$, the program \eqref{eq:subgame2def} keeps the prosumer blocks $i\in S$
and the \emph{same} common variables $(\xcommon,X_{00})$ and coupling; the common variables
are thus available to every coalition, not owned by any. The dual of the $S=N$ program is
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mu,\{\nu_i\},\nu_0}\ 0
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
G_i^{\top}\nu_i+T_i^{\top}\mu\ge \widetilde W_i\ \ (i\in N),\qquad
G_0^{\top}\nu_0+T_0^{\top}\mu\ge \widetilde W_0,
\label{eq:owendual}
\end{equation}
the zero objective reflecting the zero right-hand side of every row (all couplings
homogeneous, and the resource right-hand sides $g_i,g_0$ entering the dual objective as
$\sum_i\inner{\nu_i}{g_i}+\inner{\nu_0}{g_0}$, which we make explicit below). Let
$(\mu^{*},\{\nu_i^{*}\}_{i\in N},\nu_0^{*})$ be optimal and define the \emph{Owen
allocation over the prosumers only},
\begin{equation}
\alpha^{\mathrm{Owen}}_i:=\inner{\nu_i^{*}}{g_i}\ \ (i\in N).
\label{eq:owen}
\end{equation}
The common variables receive no share. Then $\alpha^{\mathrm{Owen}}$ lies in the core of
the prosumer game.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is Owen's theorem with common technology \citep{Owen1975,Uhan2015}. By LP strong
duality the dual is attained, with primal optimum
$\vlr_N(M,\lambda)=\sum_{i\in N}\inner{\nu_i^{*}}{g_i}+\inner{\nu_0^{*}}{g_0}$. The
common-variable description $\liftset_0$ contributes only its own RLT identities and
$\xcommon\ge0$; we take the convexity/normalization of the common block to carry the
endowment, and \emph{the prosumer allocation \eqref{eq:owen} is defined to be the
prosumer part} $\sum_{i\in N}\inner{\nu_i^{*}}{g_i}$, with any residual common-block term
$\inner{\nu_0^{*}}{g_0}$ absorbed into the grand value by efficiency. (In the model
$\xcommon$ has no nonzero endowment beyond nonnegativity, so $\inner{\nu_0^{*}}{g_0}=0$
and $\sum_{i\in N}\alpha^{\mathrm{Owen}}_i=\vlr_N$ exactly; see
Remark~\ref{rem:common-value}.) For \emph{stability}, restrict the grand-coalition dual
solution to the prosumer blocks $i\in S$, keeping $(\mu^{*},\nu_0^{*})$ unchanged. Every
constraint of \eqref{eq:subgame2def} is either internal to a single prosumer
($G_iY_i=g_i$, $x_i\ge0$), internal to the common variables ($\liftset_0$), or the shared
homogeneous coupling. The common-variable constraint $G_0^{\top}\nu_0^{*}+T_0^{\top}\mu^{*}\ge\widetilde W_0$
appears \emph{identically} in the coalition-$S$ dual --- it carries no $i$-index, because
$\xcommon$ is shared by all coalitions --- so the restriction is dual-feasible for
coalition $S$, with dual objective $\ge\vlr_S(M,\lambda)$; LP weak duality gives
$\sum_{i\in S}\alpha^{\mathrm{Owen}}_i\ge\vlr_S(M,\lambda)$. Stability and efficiency place
$\alpha^{\mathrm{Owen}}$ in the core. The mechanism is exactly that the common variables
add the \emph{same} dual row to every coalition, preserving the subset relation the Owen
argument relies on.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The relaxed game has a nonempty core]
\label{prop:balanced}
The grand-coalition relaxed game \eqref{eq:subgame2def} has a nonempty core, and an Owen
allocation $\alpha^{\mathrm{Owen}}_\lambda$ \eqref{eq:owen} is a core element:
$\sum_{i\in S}\alpha^{\mathrm{Owen}}_{\lambda,i}\ge \vlr_S(M,\lambda)$ for all $S$, with
equality at $S=N$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The relaxed game is a classical LPG with common technology; apply Lemma~\ref{lem:owen}.
Because the resource sets (prosumer and common) are polyhedra and the couplings are
linear, no Slater condition is needed --- LP strong duality holds whenever the program is
feasible and bounded, which it is ($\liftset_i$ bounds $(x_i,X_{ii})$, $\liftset_0$ bounds
$(\xcommon,X_{00})$, and the couplings are homogeneous equalities). This is a strict
simplification over the conic case: the inner production game is polyhedral, so its core
nonemptiness is Owen's theorem (with common technology), not a conic extension.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why efficiency holds even though the common variables get no share]
\label{rem:common-value}
The Owen allocation \eqref{eq:owen} assigns shares only to prosumers $i\in N$, yet the
grand value $\vlr_N$ includes the common-variable contribution $\inner{\widetilde W_0}{Y_0}$
(the reserve revenue minus peak penalty). This is consistent, not a leak: $\xcommon$ is a
shared decision, not a player, so the question ``what is $\xcommon$'s share'' does not
arise --- its surplus accrues to the prosumers who use it. Concretely, in the dual the
common-variable value is $\inner{\nu_0^{*}}{g_0}$, but $g_0$ carries only the
nonnegativity $\xcommon\ge0$ and the homogeneous coupling, whose endowments are zero, so
$\inner{\nu_0^{*}}{g_0}=0$ and the entire grand value is distributed across
$\{\alpha^{\mathrm{Owen}}_i\}_{i\in N}$. In production-game terms: the common technology is
a shared resource with zero endowment, so its surplus accrues to the players who use it,
exactly as in \citet{Uhan2015}. The reserve revenue and peak saving are thus allocated to
prosumers through the shadow prices $\mu^{*}$ on the reserve-adequacy and peak rows ---
which is the economically correct
outcome (those who provide headroom and reduce coincident peak are compensated).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why the homogeneous right-hand side is essential: nonzero $b_i$ breaks
the LPG]
\label{rem:homogeneous}
The relaxed game is a classical LPG only because the coupling
$\sum_i\Acom_i\xcom_i=0$ is \emph{homogeneous} (right-hand side $0$). This is not
cosmetic: a nonzero right-hand side destroys the production-game structure, as follows.
Suppose the coupling were $\sum_i\Acom_i\xcom_i=d$ with $d\neq0$. Its diagonal-RLT lift
is $\Tr((\Acom)^{\top}\Acom X_S)=\lVert d\rVert^{2}$, so dualizing it with $\lambda$ adds
to the Lagrangian objective the term $+\lambda\lVert d\rVert^{2}$ --- a constant that is
\emph{the same for every coalition}. The reduced value becomes
\[
\vlr_S(M,\lambda)=\sum_{i\in S}g_i(M,\lambda)\ +\ \lambda\lVert d\rVert^{2},
\qquad g_i:=\max_{(x_i,X_{ii})\in\liftset_i}\inner{\widetilde W_i}{Y_i},
\]
with the additive constant $\lambda\lVert d\rVert^{2}$ identical across all $S$ --- in
particular present for every singleton. An LPG value has no coalition-independent
constant; here one cannot be allocated. Indeed, to distribute it one would need shares
$s_i$ with $\sum_{i\in N}s_i=\lambda\lVert d\rVert^{2}$ (efficiency) yet
$\sum_{i\in S}s_i\ge\lambda\lVert d\rVert^{2}$ for every $S$ (stability); taking $S$ a
singleton forces $s_i\ge\lambda\lVert d\rVert^{2}$ for each $i$, and summing gives
$\lambda\lVert d\rVert^{2}=\sum_i s_i\ge n\,\lambda\lVert d\rVert^{2}$, i.e.\
$(n-1)\lambda\lVert d\rVert^{2}\le0$ --- impossible for $n\ge2$ unless
$\lambda\lVert d\rVert^{2}\le0$, which is not generic. So the sub-game is \emph{not} a
production game when $d\neq0$.

The consequence for modeling is sharp and worth stating as a design rule. The pleasant
LPG structure survives the addition of \emph{any} further realistic constraint provided
that constraint is of the production-game form
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i}A_i x_i=\sum_{i}b_i\qquad\text{with }b_i=0\ \ \text{(homogeneous)} .
\label{eq:lpg-form-homog}
\end{equation}
Here ``production-game form'' refers to the \emph{coupling}, not to the per-player
feasible sets: it means a constraint that is separable as $\sum_i A_ix_i=\sum_i b_i$ with
each $A_ix_i$ touching only player $i$ (the mixed-integer sets $\feas_i$ may remain
arbitrary). The additional requirement $b_i=0$ is what our construction needs: a
nonzero player endowment $b_i$ would reintroduce the $\lambda\lVert\sum_ib_i\rVert^{2}$
constant above and break the LPG. The community balance \eqref{eq:coupling} is exactly of
this form --- each carrier/period clears to zero --- and so, crucially, are the
reserve-adequacy and peak-definition rows of Section~\ref{sec:construction}: each is
homogeneous ($r^{\mathrm{sym}}-\sum_i\sum_k r^{k,\pm}_{it}\le0$ and
$\sum_i\tfrac{1}{\Delta T}(i^{E,\mathrm{mkt}}_{it}-e^{E,\mathrm{mkt}}_{it})-p\le0$, zero
right-hand side), with the common variables $r^{\mathrm{sym}},p$ entering as shared
columns rather than as a prosumer endowment. This is precisely why the reserve
and peak extensions preserve the LPG structure (and is verified at the level of the lift
in Lemma~\ref{lem:vanish}: the prosumer--common cross blocks vanish, and no constant
appears because every row's right-hand side is zero). A constraint with a genuine nonzero
aggregate --- a fixed external demand the community as a whole must meet, say --- would
fall outside the construction and require separate treatment. The dividing line is exactly
homogeneity: shared resources priced at the margin (reserve, peak, internal balance) are
admissible; fixed exogenous targets are not.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The LRB allocation is budget-balanced at $\vmip$; $\vchp$ is not a
property of our allocation]
\label{rem:Mcone}
We state the main point first, because it is easy to lose. At the dual-\emph{optimal}
multiplier $(M^{*},\lambda^{*})$ --- the one the LRB actually uses --- the allocation
$\chi$ is \emph{budget-balanced at $\vmip$} (Theorem~\ref{thm:liu}):
\begin{equation*}
\sum_{i\in N}\chi_i=\vlr_N(M^{*},\lambda^{*})=\vmip(N).
\end{equation*}
The first equality is efficiency of an LPG core (the core lies on the grand
value of that game); the second is conic strong duality
$\min_{M,\lambda}\vlr_N=\vmip(N)$. So the total distributed is exactly $\vmip(N)$, not
$\vchp(N)$. The convex-hull value $\vchp$ \emph{does not appear} in the LRB
allocation's budget.

Why $\vchp$ keeps being mentioned, and why it is a red herring here. The value
$\vlr_N(M,\lambda)$ at a \emph{generic, non-optimal} multiplier is a convex-hull-type
quantity: by Lemma~\ref{lem:vanish} the cross blocks drop out, and per player
$\max_{(x_i,X_{ii})\in\liftset_i}\inner{\widetilde W_i}{Y_i}=\max_{x_i\in\conv(\feas_i)}(\cdots)$
by Burer at the per-player level (Lemma~\ref{lem:proj} below), so
$\vlr_N(M,\lambda)\ge\vmip(N)$ with the inequality typically strict. If one
\emph{stopped at a generic $M$}, the LPG core would distribute that larger
amount and over-pay relative to $\vmip(N)$ --- stable but not efficient, i.e.\ in the
core of the convex-hull game, not of $\Gamma^{\mathrm{MIP}}$. But the LRB does
\emph{not} stop at a generic $M$; it minimizes over $M$, and the minimization walks
the budget down from the convex-hull level to $\vmip(N)$. Thus $\vchp$ is the value
at a \emph{generic} multiplier, a way-station the dual passes through, not the budget
of the final allocation. The companion program of Section~\ref{sec:chp} is precisely
this way-station, made into a standalone object and identified with convex hull
pricing.

The price of this is that an \emph{attained} optimal multiplier is required for the
\emph{exact} $\vmip$ budget; a generic one over-distributes, and finding even an
$\varepsilon$-optimal one is the \textsf{NP}-hard step (Open point~\ref{q:comp}). When the
optimum is not attained (Open point~\ref{q:attain}), the budget is $\vmip(N)$ only up to
$\varepsilon$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:liu}(ii)). Either way this is a cost in
\emph{computation} and in \emph{attainment}, not a collapse to a convex-hull object: the
budget is $\vmip(N)$ (exactly, or within $\varepsilon$), never $\vchp(N)$.
\end{remark}

\section{Composition: the LPG core is a core of the mixed-integer game}
\label{sec:composition}

\paragraph{No sub-game split: a single relaxed game.}
Liu's composition would split the relaxed characteristic function into two sub-games and
sum their allocations \citep[][Thm.~1]{LiuEtAl}; as explained in
Section~\ref{sec:construction}, that split rests on a coalition-indexed right-hand side
$\gamma_s$ which our homogeneous coupling does not have. There is therefore no
decomposition to perform here: by Lemma~\ref{lem:vanish} the dualized objective is
already block-separable, and the relaxed Lagrangian \eqref{eq:subgame2def} is a
\emph{single} linear production game. We obtain a stable allocation directly from this
one game --- the mechanism Liu uses within each sub-game --- rather than by composing two.

\paragraph{One Owen allocation, but not a polynomial one.}
The relaxed game \eqref{eq:subgame2def} is a linear production game over the polyhedral
resource sets $\liftset_i$ with a single linear coupling, so by Owen's theorem
(Lemma~\ref{lem:owen}, Proposition~\ref{prop:balanced}) a core allocation is read off
directly from \emph{one} dual solution of the grand-coalition LPG --- the community
prices on the coupling and the block duals on $\liftset_i$. No enumeration of coalitions
and no column generation are needed: a \emph{single} Owen solution suffices.

This is a structural simplification, \emph{not} a complexity reduction. Although the
inner production game is polyhedral once a multiplier is fixed, obtaining the
\emph{optimal} multiplier $(M^{*},\lambda^{*})$ requires solving the dual \eqref{eq:dual},
i.e.\ optimizing the matrix multiplier $M$ over the copositive cone $\COP$, which is
\textsf{NP}-hard (Open point~\ref{q:comp}). What the LPG structure buys is that, given the
multiplier, the allocation is a single Owen solution rather than a coalition-indexed
family --- the difficulty is concentrated in the \emph{outer} copositive solve over $M$,
not in enumerating coalitions. We return to this in Open point~\ref{q:comp}.

\paragraph{Strong duality holds; dual attainment is the delicate point.}
With the per-player cone removed (Remark~\ref{rem:no-perplayer-cone}), the construction
rests on the strong duality of the lifted grand-coalition program --- and, for
\emph{efficiency}, on the \emph{attainment} of a dual minimizer. These two are not the
same, and recent work shows the second can fail. We separate them.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.4em,style=nextline]
\item[\textnormal{(A1) Zero duality gap.}] The CPP \eqref{eq:cpp} and its copositive dual
have no duality gap: $\inf_{M\in\COP,\lambda}\vlr_N(M,\lambda)=\vmip(N)$. By
\citet[Thm.~3]{CifuentesDeyXu2025} this holds whenever the per-player feasible region is
\emph{bounded} or the objective is \emph{convex} (linear, in our case) --- both true
here, so (A1) is automatic, with no need for a primal interior point.
\item[\textnormal{(A2) Dual attainment.}] The infimum in (A1) is \emph{attained} by some
$(M^{*},\lambda^{*})\in\COP\times\R$.
\end{description}

\paragraph{A caution on (A2): the dual optimum need not exist.}
It is tempting to assume (A2) follows from (A1) by a Slater argument on the primal
CPP. It does not, and this is the subtle gap in the present construction.
\citet[Thm.~3(d)]{CifuentesDeyXu2025} exhibit a bounded MBQP (the stable-set CPP of a
$6$-clique) for which strong duality holds yet \emph{the copositive dual optimum is not
attained}; they note further that, in general, the primal CPP \eqref{eq:cpp} has \emph{no}
Slater (interior) point even when the feasible region is bounded. In our setting this is
not merely ``not automatic'' but structurally impossible: any binary identity $z=z^2$
makes the lifted matrix singular at every feasible point, so $\mathrm{CPP}(N)$ has no
interior whatsoever (Section~\ref{sec:slater-structural},
Proposition~\ref{prop:no-interior}), and Burer's reduction step recovers one only for a
single equality \citep[Prop.~16]{BomzeGabl2023}. So the route ``primal Slater
$\Rightarrow$ dual attainment'' is blocked at the source, not just the weaker ``bounded
$\Rightarrow$ primal Slater'': boundedness gives
the gap (A1) but not the attainment (A2). What boundedness \emph{does} guarantee is, for
every $\varepsilon>0$, a closed-form $\varepsilon$-optimal dual solution
$(M^{\varepsilon},\lambda^{\varepsilon})$ with
$\vlr_N(M^{\varepsilon},\lambda^{\varepsilon})\le\vmip(N)+\varepsilon$
\citep[Thm.~3(b)]{CifuentesDeyXu2025}.

\paragraph{This gap is not specific to us; it is latent in copositive pricing generally.}
The same issue is present in \citet{GuoEtAl}, whose copositive-duality pricing for unit
commitment fixes ``all primal and dual variables at the optimal values'' and invokes
strong duality \citep[via][]{CifuentesDeyXu2025} without separately establishing that a
dual \emph{optimum} exists. Their cutting-plane implementation in fact terminates with an
$\varepsilon$-optimal copositive solution, i.e.\ it computes an $\varepsilon$-price; the
exactness is asserted at the level of strong duality (the gap), not of attainment. We
therefore treat (A2) as a genuine hypothesis to be discharged \emph{per instance}, not a
free consequence of boundedness, and we state our main result in two forms accordingly:
an exact-core statement \emph{conditional} on (A2), and an unconditional
$\varepsilon$-core statement that needs only (A1).

We first record the coalition-wise upper bound, then state the theorem.

\begin{corollary}[Coalition-wise upper bound]
\label{cor:upper}
For every $S\subseteq N$ and every $(M,\lambda)$ with $M\in\COP$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:finiteness}, $\ \vlr_S(M,\lambda)\ge\vmip(S)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\vlr_S(M,\lambda)$ is a maximization whose feasible set contains the CP-feasible set of
the coalition-$S$ program \eqref{eq:cpp} (the dualized constraints (d),(e) were moved to
the objective). On any CP-feasible $Y_S$ of \eqref{eq:cpp}: the dualized cone term gives
$\inner{M}{Y_S}\ge0$ (as $Y_S\in\CP$, $M\in\COP$) and the dualized squared coupling gives
$-\lambda\Tr((\Acom)^{\top}\Acom X_S)=0$ (it holds there). So the Lagrangian objective is
$\ge\inner{W_S}{Y_S}$, giving $\vlr_S(M,\lambda)\ge\vmip(S)$. This is weak duality, valid
at \emph{all} admissible multipliers --- the analogue of Liu's $c^{\mathrm{LR}}(S;\lambda)\le c(S)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Mixed-integer core, exact and approximate]
\label{thm:liu}
Assume \textnormal{(A1)} (which holds here, the feasible region being bounded).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \emph{(Exact core, conditional on \textnormal{(A2)}.)} If additionally a dual
optimum $(M^{*},\lambda^{*})$ is attained, then the Owen allocation
$\chi:=\alpha^{\mathrm{Owen}}$ of the relaxed LPG \eqref{eq:subgame2def} at
$(M^{*},\lambda^{*})$ is in the core of $\Gamma^{\mathrm{MIP}}$:
\[
\sum_{i\in S}\chi_i\ \ge\ \vmip(S)\ \ \forall S\subseteq N,
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\sum_{i\in N}\chi_i\ =\ \vmip(N).
\]
\item \emph{(Approximate core, unconditional.)} For every $\varepsilon>0$, the Owen
allocation $\chi^{\varepsilon}$ at an $\varepsilon$-optimal multiplier
$(M^{\varepsilon},\lambda^{\varepsilon})$ \citep[Thm.~3(b)]{CifuentesDeyXu2025} satisfies
\[
\sum_{i\in S}\chi^{\varepsilon}_i\ \ge\ \vmip(S)\ \ \forall S\subseteq N,
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\vmip(N)\ \le\ \sum_{i\in N}\chi^{\varepsilon}_i\ \le\ \vmip(N)+\varepsilon,
\]
i.e.\ it is stable and $\varepsilon$-efficient --- an $\varepsilon$-core allocation.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{(i).} At the fixed multiplier $(M^{*},\lambda^{*})$, Lemma~\ref{lem:owen} produces
the Owen allocation $\chi$, in the core of the relaxed game:
$\sum_{i\in S}\chi_i\ge\vlr_S(M^{*},\lambda^{*})$ for all $S$, with equality at $S=N$.
\emph{Stability:} chaining with Corollary~\ref{cor:upper},
$\sum_{i\in S}\chi_i\ge\vlr_S(M^{*},\lambda^{*})\ge\vmip(S)$. \emph{Efficiency:} at $S=N$,
$\sum_{i\in N}\chi_i=\vlr_N(M^{*},\lambda^{*})$, which by (A1)$+$(A2) equals $\vmip(N)$.
Hence $\chi$ is in the core, and the total distributed is exactly $\vmip(N)$, never
$\vchp(N)$.

\emph{(ii).} The same Owen construction at $(M^{\varepsilon},\lambda^{\varepsilon})$
gives, by Lemma~\ref{lem:owen}, an allocation with
$\sum_{i\in S}\chi^{\varepsilon}_i\ge\vlr_S(M^{\varepsilon},\lambda^{\varepsilon})$ for all
$S$ and equality at $S=N$. Stability follows as before via Corollary~\ref{cor:upper}
(valid at every admissible multiplier, optimal or not):
$\sum_{i\in S}\chi^{\varepsilon}_i\ge\vlr_S(M^{\varepsilon},\lambda^{\varepsilon})\ge\vmip(S)$.
For the budget, $\sum_{i\in N}\chi^{\varepsilon}_i=\vlr_N(M^{\varepsilon},\lambda^{\varepsilon})$,
which lies in $[\vmip(N),\vmip(N)+\varepsilon]$: the lower bound is
Corollary~\ref{cor:upper} at $S=N$, the upper bound is $\varepsilon$-optimality. Thus
$\chi^{\varepsilon}$ is stable and overpays by at most $\varepsilon$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Reading the two parts: which one is the theorem?]
\label{rem:which-part}
Part (ii) is unconditional and is what the construction \emph{guarantees}: a stable
allocation whose budget exceeds $\vmip(N)$ by an arbitrarily small $\varepsilon$. Part (i)
is the cleaner statement but is \emph{conditional} on (A2), dual attainment, which ---
contrary to what a Slater argument would suggest --- does not follow from boundedness
(Cifuentes--Dey--Xu, Thm.~3(d)) and must be checked for the specific community instance
(Open point~\ref{q:attain}). When (A2) fails, there is no exact dual optimum to evaluate
$\alpha^{\mathrm{Owen}}$ at, and part (i) is vacuous; part (ii) still delivers an
$\varepsilon$-core. The honest headline is therefore: \emph{the community game has a
nonempty $\varepsilon$-core for every $\varepsilon>0$, and a nonempty (exact) core
whenever the copositive dual optimum is attained.}

\emph{Why a generic multiplier is not enough.} If $(M,\lambda)$ is dual-feasible but
neither optimal nor $\varepsilon$-optimal, stability still holds (Corollary~\ref{cor:upper}),
but the budget $\vlr_N(M,\lambda)$ may exceed $\vmip(N)$ by an uncontrolled amount, up to
the convex-hull value $\vchp(N)$: the allocation then lies in the core of the convex-hull
game, not (even approximately) of $\Gamma^{\mathrm{MIP}}$. The $\varepsilon$-optimal
multiplier is what bounds the overpayment by $\varepsilon$.
\end{remark}

\section{A companion identity: per-player copositive lift equals convex hull pricing}
\label{sec:chp}

The construction above dualizes the joint cone \emph{and} the squared coupling, and
arrives at $\vmip$. We now record what happens under the opposite choice --- \emph{not}
dualizing, and lifting \emph{only} the per-player feasible sets --- and show it reproduces
convex hull pricing exactly. This identifies the ``way-station'' of
Remark~\ref{rem:Mcone} as a standalone, well-known object, and connects the present note
to the convex-hull-pricing allocation of the companion manuscript.

\paragraph{The per-player-lifted program.}
Take the community-balance LP, keep the linear coupling, drop the squared coupling (d)
and the joint cone (e), and replace each per-player feasible set by its own completely
positive lift --- i.e.\ impose the per-player cone $Y_i\in\CP_i$ together with the affine
$\liftset_i$, block by block:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{(P)}:\qquad
\max_{\{Y_i\}}\ \Big\{\sum_{i\in N}\inner{W_i}{Y_i}\ :\
\sum_{i\in N}\Acom_i\xcom_i=0,\quad (x_i,X_{ii})\in\liftset_i,\ \ Y_i\in\CP_i\ \ \forall i\Big\}.
\label{eq:Pchp}
\end{equation}
Here no cross-block variable $X_{ij}$ appears at all (the squared coupling, the only term
that ever introduced them, is absent), and each block is constrained only within itself.
This is \emph{not} the program of Section~\ref{sec:construction}; it is the
per-player-decoupled lift, and we claim it equals convex hull pricing.

\paragraph{Per-player Burer at the level of sets.}
The engine is that the per-player completely positive lift recovers the convex hull of
$\feas_i$ not merely in optimal value but as a projected set. We isolate this because the
coupling makes a value-only statement insufficient (the blocks cannot be optimized
separately under the shared balance).

\begin{lemma}[Per-player lift projects onto the convex hull]
\label{lem:proj}
For each $i$, with $\feas_i$ bounded mixed-integer in the slacked form of Step~1,
\begin{equation}
\proj_{x_i}\big\{(x_i,X_{ii}):\ (x_i,X_{ii})\in\liftset_i,\ \ Y_i\in\CP_i\big\}
\ =\ \conv(\feas_i).
\label{eq:proj}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Burer's reformulation (Theorem~\ref{thm:burer} with $K=\R_+^{n_i}$, applied to player
$i$'s data alone) states that for \emph{every} linear objective $w_i$,
\[
\max\{w_i^{\top}x_i:\ x_i\in\feas_i\}
=\max\{\inner{W_i}{Y_i}:\ (x_i,X_{ii})\in\liftset_i,\ Y_i\in\CP_i\},
\]
the right-hand feasible set being convex. Equality of the optimal value for every linear
objective $w_i$ means the two convex sets have the same support function in every
direction of the $x_i$-coordinates; since the lifted feasible set is closed and convex,
its projection onto the $x_i$-coordinates is the closed convex set with that support
function, namely $\conv(\feas_i)$ \citep[supporting-hyperplane characterization; see
also][Thm.~6, for the face-based set identity]{BomzeGabl2023}. (The objective $W_i$ has
no $X_{ij}$ or off-diagonal-$X_{ii}$ component \eqref{eq:Wmat}, so the support function in
the $x_i$-directions is unaffected by the lifted entries; these are exactly the
``certificate'' coordinates that make $x_i\in\conv(\feas_i)$ representable.)
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[\eqref{eq:Pchp} is convex hull pricing]
\label{prop:chp}
The per-player-lifted program \eqref{eq:Pchp} has the same optimal value, and the same
set of optimal $\{x_i\}$ and community-balance multipliers, as convex hull pricing
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{(CHP)}:\qquad
\vchp(N)=\max_{\{x_i\}}\Big\{\sum_{i\in N}w_i^{\top}x_i:\
\sum_{i\in N}\Acom_i\xcom_i=0,\ \ x_i\in\conv(\feas_i)\ \forall i\Big\}.
\label{eq:CHP}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The objective $\sum_i\inner{W_i}{Y_i}=\sum_iw_i^{\top}x_i$ and the coupling
$\sum_i\Acom_i\xcom_i=0$ of \eqref{eq:Pchp} depend on the lifted variables \emph{only}
through the first-row coordinates $x_i$; no $X_{ii}$ off-diagonal entry and no $X_{ij}$
appears in either (the squared coupling is absent and $W_i$ is the pure first-order
matrix \eqref{eq:Wmat}). The lifted variables $\{X_{ii}\}$ are therefore free except for
their role inside the per-player constraints $\{(x_i,X_{ii})\in\liftset_i,\,Y_i\in\CP_i\}$,
which couple to nothing outside block $i$. Projecting each block onto its $x_i$-coordinates
is thus valid even under the shared balance --- the balance constrains only the $\{x_i\}$,
which survive the projection unchanged --- and by Lemma~\ref{lem:proj} the projected
per-player feasible set is exactly $\conv(\feas_i)$. Hence \eqref{eq:Pchp} reduces to
\eqref{eq:CHP}, with identical optimal $\{x_i\}$; and since the balance constraint is
carried verbatim through the projection, its optimal dual multipliers coincide. This is
the statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Reading: why (P) is $\vchp$ while the main construction is $\vmip$]
\label{rem:chp-vs-mip}
The two programs differ in exactly one decision: whether the cross-player coupling is
lifted and dualized.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{Section~\ref{sec:construction} (main).} The squared coupling (d) is
\emph{retained and dualized}; the cross blocks $X_{ij}$ then \emph{cancel} (the
$(\mathrm{c},\mathrm{c})$ entry against $\lambda(\Acom_i)^\top\Acom_j$,
Remark~\ref{rem:twomech}), so the inter-player information is preserved through $\lambda$.
At the optimal multiplier the value is $\vmip(N)$.
\item \emph{Section~\ref{sec:chp} (companion).} The squared coupling is \emph{never
introduced}; there is no cross block to cancel, and each player is independently relaxed
to $\conv(\feas_i)$. The value is $\vchp(N)\ge\vmip(N)$.
\end{itemize}
So $\vchp$ is what one gets by lifting per-player but \emph{declining} to couple the
lifts; $\vmip$ is what one gets by coupling them (through the squared balance) and paying
the price of an outer copositive solve. The companion identity therefore places convex
hull pricing precisely: it is the per-player-decoupled corner of the same copositive
machinery, and it coincides with the convex-hull-pricing allocation analyzed in the main
manuscript. In particular, when $\vchp(N)=\vmip(N)$ (zero integrality gap) the two
constructions agree and convex hull pricing already lands in the core of
$\Gamma^{\mathrm{MIP}}$; when the gap is positive they diverge, and only the coupled
construction reaches $\vmip$.
\end{remark}

\section{The homogeneity requirement: scope, necessity, and inhomogeneous couplings}
\label{sec:homogeneity}

The construction's one structural demand on the couplings is that they be
\emph{homogeneous} (zero right-hand side), $\sum_iA_ix_i+\Azero\xcommon=0$
(Remark~\ref{rem:homogeneous}). This section does three things: it shows the requirement
is the \emph{norm}, not the exception, in energy-community models; it shows the
requirement is \emph{necessary} for our method, not an artifact we could engineer away;
and it records what can still be salvaged when a coupling is genuinely inhomogeneous.

\subsection{Homogeneity is the norm in energy-community coupling}

Internal community exchange is, physically, a \emph{conservation law}: every unit one
member injects is a unit another withdraws, so the clearing nets to zero. This makes the
central couplings homogeneous across the energy-community literature, not by assumption
but by the structure of trade. Table~\ref{tab:homog} collects the clearing/coupling
constraints of representative community-market models; in each, the inter-player coupling
has zero right-hand side.

The practical reading is that the homogeneity requirement aligns with --- rather than
restricts --- the modeling conventions of the field: a community market clears internally
precisely because production and consumption offset, and that offsetting \emph{is} the
zero right-hand side. Where nonzero data appear (a household's fixed demand, a
net-metering threshold), they sit in the prosumer-private region $\feas_i$, which the
lift handles in full; they do not enter the coupling.

\subsection{Homogeneity is necessary for the method, not incidental}

One might hope to absorb a nonzero right-hand side by some reformulation --- penalizing
it, relaxing the cone, or cutting it away --- and recover the LPG structure. None of these
works, for a single structural reason, which we state once and then illustrate by the
routes it rules out.

\begin{proposition}[The inhomogeneity lives where no cone or relocation can reach it]
\label{prop:inhomog-wall}
Consider a coupling $\sum_iA_ix_i=\sum_id_i$ with $\sum_id_i\neq0$. Squaring it for the
RLT lift produces
\[
\Big(\sum_iA_ix_i-\sum_id_i\Big)^2
=\underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{i,j}(A_ix_i)^{\top}(A_jx_j)}_{\text{quadratic: lifts to }X_{ij}}
\ -\ \underbrace{2\Big(\sum_id_i\Big)^{\!\top}\!\sum_jA_jx_j}_{\text{linear in }x}
\ +\ \underbrace{\Big\|\sum_id_i\Big\|^{2}}_{\text{constant}} .
\]
Only the quadratic part lifts into the matrix blocks $X_{ij}$; the linear and constant
parts occupy the first-order and constant components of the lifted objective. A
completely positive (or SDP, doubly-nonnegative, RLT) relaxation constrains only the
matrix block $X$; it cannot alter the linear or constant parts, which are fixed by the
data $\{d_i\}$. Consequently the coalition value carries an additive
$-\lambda\lVert\sum_{i\in S}d_i\rVert^{2}$, whose expansion contains cross terms
$-2\lambda\,d_i^{\top}d_j$ that are attributable to no single player and present in every
coalition containing both $i$ and $j$. No such value is additively separable, so the
reduced game is not a linear production game.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The decomposition is direct expansion; the lift sends $x_ix_j^{\top}\mapsto X_{ij}$ and
leaves the data products $d_i^{\top}d_j$ and the linear term untouched, since these
contain no decision variable that the cone constrains. The cross-term obstruction to
additive separability is the computation of Remark~\ref{rem:homogeneous}, now with
$\sum_{i\in S}d_i$ in place of a single aggregate.
\end{proof}

This single fact disposes of the natural workarounds, each of which fails for the same
reason --- the inhomogeneity is not a cone phenomenon:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{Dualize the squared coupling} (our method): the cross term
$-2\lambda d_i^{\top}d_j$ remains in the value; not an LPG.
\item \emph{Put the squared penalty in the objective with a fixed weight $\rho$}: the
same decomposition holds with $\rho$ for $\lambda$; the linear and constant parts are
identical, and the per-coalition linear coefficient $2\rho\sum_{i\in S}d_i$ already
depends on $S$. Relocating the squared term from constraint to objective changes nothing.
\item \emph{Tune $\rho$ for a zero gap}: this rescales the offending terms but does not
remove them; and it coincides with the dual minimization we already perform, so it
inherits the same \textsf{NP}-hardness and attainment question.
\item \emph{Relax the cone (SDP, DNN, RLT)}: these enlarge or replace the cone that $X$
lives in; the constant $d_i^{\top}d_j$ and the linear term are outside $X$ and are
untouched. (Cone relaxation addresses the \emph{quadratic} nonconvexity, which here is
already neutralized by the dual matrix $M$; it does not address inhomogeneity.)
\item \emph{Drop the coupling and add Benders feasibility cuts}: a feasibility cut has the
form $\sum_i(u^{\top}T_i)x_i\ge u^{\top}h$, homogeneous iff the recourse right-hand side
$h=0$. If $h\neq0$ the cuts are themselves inhomogeneous (the obstruction returns), and
they are also a \emph{looser} outer approximation than placing the row directly in the
CPP --- so they cost exactness while buying nothing. If $h=0$ the original coupling was
already homogeneous and admissible directly. The detour is therefore either ineffective
or unnecessary.
\end{itemize}
The conclusion is that homogeneity is close to a \emph{necessary} condition for the method,
not a convenience: any genuinely nonzero aggregate target breaks the production-game
structure, and no relocation or convex relaxation of the lift repairs it.

\subsection{What survives for a genuinely inhomogeneous coupling}

When a model does carry a true nonzero aggregate target (a fixed external obligation the
community as a whole must meet, distinct from internal clearing), two honest options
remain, neither of which is the clean exact-core result of the homogeneous case.

\paragraph{(i) Keep exactness, lose the LPG.}
Placing the inhomogeneous row directly in the CPP keeps the lift exact for $\vmip$
(Burer's theorem applies to the full system, nonzero right-hand side included). The
resulting grand-coalition value is still $\vmip(N)$, but the reduced Lagrangian is no
longer a production game on that row, so the Owen shortcut is unavailable: a core element
must be obtained from the general core program (or shown empty) rather than read from a
single dual solution. The copositive lift still \emph{certifies} the value; it no longer
\emph{hands back} the allocation.

\paragraph{(ii) Keep the LPG, accept an $\varepsilon$-core.}
Alternatively one may form the Owen allocation from the \emph{uniform} (per-player,
diagonal) part of the dual payment and discard the cross-term payment
$-2\lambda d_i^{\top}d_j$ --- exactly the move \citet{GuoEtAl} make when they price with
uniform prices and set aside the non-uniform (off-diagonal) payments associated with the
lifted balance. Writing $\chi_i$ for this uniform allocation, the core conditions hold up
to the discarded cross sum:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i\in S}\chi_i-\vlr_S(M,\lambda)
\ =\ 2\lambda\!\!\sum_{\substack{i<j\\ i,j\in S}}\! d_i^{\top}d_j\ =:\ E_S ,
\label{eq:eps-core-gap}
\end{equation}
so $\chi$ is stable and efficient \emph{up to} $E_S$ --- an $\varepsilon$-core with
$\varepsilon=\max_S|E_S|$ controlled by the inhomogeneity $\{d_i\}$ and the multiplier
$\lambda$. Two regimes are worth distinguishing. When the endowments share a sign (e.g.\
all members net-importing the external target), $E_S\ge0$ and $\chi$ is stable but
over-distributes (budget above $\vmip(N)$); when signs are mixed, stability itself can
fail for coalitions where negative cross terms dominate. As $\lVert d\rVert\to0$ (the
coupling approaches the homogeneous conservation case) the gap $E_S\to0$ and the exact
core is recovered.

\paragraph{The parallel with Guo et al.}
Option (ii) is the cooperative-core analogue of \citeauthor{GuoEtAl}'s pricing guarantee.
Their uniform-price mechanism does not achieve exact individual rationality for every
generator; via a Shapley--Folkman argument it secures it for all but at most $|T|+1$ of
them, with the system operator absorbing the residual. We have no system operator to
absorb a residual in a core, so the discarded cross payment surfaces instead as the
$\varepsilon$ in \eqref{eq:eps-core-gap}. In both cases the exact guarantee is recovered
in the favorable limit --- convexity/large-system for their individual rationality, and
homogeneity ($\lVert d\rVert\to0$) for our core --- and in both cases the gap is
explicitly bounded by a measure of the problem's departure from that limit. This places
the homogeneity requirement in context: it is the cooperative-game counterpart of the
conditions under which copositive \emph{pricing} is known to be exact, and it fails
gracefully, with a quantified $\varepsilon$, rather than catastrophically.

\section{What is settled, what is assumed, what is open}

\paragraph{Settled by structure (no copositive program solved).}
Lemma~\ref{lem:vanish} (cross blocks vanish in value; explicit coefficient computation),
\eqref{eq:subgame2def} (the reduced Lagrangian is a classical LPG),
Lemma~\ref{lem:owen} and Proposition~\ref{prop:balanced} (the relaxed game has a nonempty
core, by the Owen restriction), Corollary~\ref{cor:upper} (coalition-wise upper bound,
weak duality), Theorem~\ref{thm:liu} (the composition into a mixed-integer core
allocation), and --- for the companion identity --- Lemma~\ref{lem:proj} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:chp} (per-player lift $=$ convex hull pricing). These are structural
facts about production games and Lagrangians; none requires solving a copositive program.

\paragraph{The hypotheses the theorem rests on.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:liu} separates into two layers, on the lifted grand-coalition program:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(A\arabic*)},leftmargin=2.9em]
\item \textbf{Zero duality gap.} $\inf_{M\in\COP,\lambda}\vlr_N(M,\lambda)=\vmip(N)$. This
holds whenever the feasible region is bounded or the objective is convex
\citep[Thm.~3]{CifuentesDeyXu2025}, both true here, so (A1) is \emph{automatic} --- no
structural condition, and notably \emph{no primal interior}, is needed. (A1) alone gives
the unconditional $\varepsilon$-core, Theorem~\ref{thm:liu}(ii).
\item \textbf{Dual attainment.} The infimum in (A1) is achieved by some
$(M^{*},\lambda^{*})$. This is \emph{not} implied by boundedness
\citep[Thm.~3(d)]{CifuentesDeyXu2025} and is the genuine hypothesis; under it,
Theorem~\ref{thm:liu}(i) gives an exact core. A primal interior (Slater) point of
\eqref{eq:cpp} would be sufficient, but is \emph{unavailable}: Burer-type lifts have no
primal interior once binaries are present (Section~\ref{sec:slater-structural},
Proposition~\ref{prop:no-interior}). Attainment is therefore left as an instance-dependent
condition --- equivalent to termination of a copositive cutting-plane solver
(Section~\ref{sec:slater-structural}, Open point~\ref{q:attain}).
\end{enumerate}
The inner production game needs \emph{no} Slater condition of its own: it is polyhedral,
so LP strong duality applies automatically (Proposition~\ref{prop:balanced}) --- this is
the simplification from removing the per-player cones. We stress two limits. First, the
exact-$\vmip$ statement is the value at $N$ at an \emph{attained} optimum $M^{*}$; at a
generic or $\varepsilon$-optimal multiplier the budget is $\vmip(N)$ up to $\varepsilon$
(or, generically, up to the convex hull), so attainment is what makes it exact
(Remark~\ref{rem:Mcone}, Remark~\ref{rem:which-part}). Second, what $M^{*}$ does to
\emph{proper}-coalition values $\vlr_S(M^{*})$ is undetermined here, and that is what
decides whether the result exceeds the standard ``balanced convex hull $\Rightarrow$
nonempty integer core'' fact.

\paragraph{Genuinely open.}
\begin{question}[Dual attainment for the community CPP --- exact vs.\ $\varepsilon$ core]
\label{q:attain}
The exact-core part of Theorem~\ref{thm:liu}(i) needs (A2): the copositive dual optimum
$(M^{*},\lambda^{*})$ is attained. By \citet[Thm.~3(d)]{CifuentesDeyXu2025} this is
\emph{not} automatic for bounded MBQPs --- their $6$-clique stable-set instance has
strong duality but no dual optimum. The natural sufficient condition, a primal interior
(Slater) point of $\mathrm{CPP}(N)$ \eqref{eq:cpp}, is \emph{structurally unavailable}
here: any binary identity $z=z^2$ makes the lifted matrix singular at every feasible point
(Proposition~\ref{prop:no-interior}), so $\mathrm{CPP}(N)$ has no interior, and Burer's
reduction step recovers one only for a single equality constraint
\citep[Prop.~16]{BomzeGabl2023}, which the multi-row community CPP is not. Attainment must
therefore be argued, if at all, by a route that does not pass through a primal interior ---
for instance a coercivity or closedness property of the copositive dual that forces the
infimum to be achieved. Equivalently, by the algorithmic mirror of
Section~\ref{sec:slater-structural}, attainment holds exactly when a copositive
cutting-plane solver terminates on the instance; on non-terminating instances the dual
value still converges to $\vmip(N)$ but no exact optimizer exists. Whether the
sector-coupled community model falls in the terminating (attained) class is the open
question; we do not resolve it. Absent (A2), the construction still yields the
unconditional $\varepsilon$-core of Theorem~\ref{thm:liu}(ii); the open point is precisely
what upgrades $\varepsilon$-core to exact core. We regard this as the central theoretical
gap, shared with existing copositive-pricing work (\citealp{GuoEtAl}, whose mechanism
likewise returns exact prices only on terminating instances), and not previously isolated
there.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Computation, and the size of the dualized copositive program]
\label{q:comp}
Theorem~\ref{thm:liu} is an \emph{existence} result. Producing the allocation
$\chi=\alpha^{\mathrm{Owen}}$ requires an (exact or $\varepsilon$-)optimal solution of the
dual, i.e.\ optimizing the matrix multiplier $M$ over the copositive cone $\COP$ together
with $\lambda$, which is \textsf{NP}-hard. A practical route is the cutting-plane /
outer-approximation scheme of \citet{GuoEtAl}, which optimizes over $\COP$ by separating
copositivity violations; this is the natural solver here, and (consistently with
Open point~\ref{q:attain}) it returns an $\varepsilon$-optimal multiplier, hence an
$\varepsilon$-core allocation, on termination.

A structural note on \emph{scale}: the dual variable $M$ is conjugate to the \emph{joint}
lifted matrix $Y_S$, whose dimension is $1+\sum_i n_i$ --- it grows with the total number
of (lifted, slacked) variables across \emph{all} prosumers. The separation oracle must
therefore certify copositivity of an object that scales with the whole community.
Crucially, with the per-player cones \emph{removed} (Remark~\ref{rem:no-perplayer-cone}),
the only copositive constraint is the single joint one --- there is no longer an
additional copositive multiplier per player block, as an earlier version carried. This
removes the $n$-fold growth in the number of copositive constraints that the redundant
per-player cones would have incurred; the remaining obstacle is the single
joint-matrix copositivity certificate, whose dimension still scales with the community.
Whether the cutting-plane scheme remains tractable at community scale is open and, in our
view, the main practical obstacle.

Any tractable inner approximation (doubly nonnegative, RLT) replaces $\CP$ by a superset,
which \emph{enlarges} the lifted value and yields an outer game whose core is contained in
--- and generally larger than --- the target; the guarantee then weakens to that
approximation's level, exactly as for convex-hull pricing. Exactness is tied to using
$\CP$ itself.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Consistency with empty-core instances]
\label{q:parity}
When the core of $\Gamma^{\mathrm{MIP}}$ is empty --- e.g.\ a parity obstruction where
$\vmip(N)$ is infeasible while proper coalitions are finite --- Theorem~\ref{thm:liu}
must not fire. It does not: in that case \eqref{eq:cpp} for $N$ is infeasible, its dual
is unbounded ($\dual^{*}(N)=+\infty$), and \emph{no} finite-value admissible multiplier
exists, so the hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:liu} is not met. The construction
correctly declines to produce an allocation rather than fabricating a $\vchp$ one. This
is a consistency check, not a theorem, but it shows the existence hypothesis is doing
real work: it is met exactly when the core is plausibly nonempty.
\end{question}

\section{Dual attainment, the primal Slater obstruction, and what is computable}
\label{sec:slater-structural}

This section addresses the one hypothesis the construction cannot obtain for free,
\emph{dual attainment} (A2) --- the existence of an \emph{attained} copositive dual optimum
$(M^\star,\lambda^\star)$, which is what upgrades the unconditional $\varepsilon$-core
(Theorem~\ref{thm:liu}(ii)) to an exact core (Theorem~\ref{thm:liu}(i)). An earlier
draft of this note claimed that the community's bidirectional trade structure supplies a
\emph{primal interior (Slater) point} of the copositive lift, which would imply (A2). That
claim was wrong, and the reason it is wrong is instructive: it pins down exactly why an
exact core is attainment-conditional while an $\varepsilon$-core is not.

\subsection{What is not at issue: value and the $\varepsilon$-core}

It is worth separating two questions that the term ``solving the copositive program''
conflates.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{The optimal value.} Whether the copositive dual \emph{value} equals
$\vmip(N)$. This is the zero-gap question (A1), and it is settled by boundedness alone:
\citet[Thm.~3(a)]{CifuentesDeyXu2025} give a strictly copositive feasible point of the
dual whenever the primal feasible region is bounded, hence a dual Slater point and zero
gap, with no structural input. The community CPP is bounded, so
$\val(\mathrm{COP})=\val(\mathrm{CPP})=\vmip(N)$ unconditionally.
\item \emph{An attained dual optimizer.} Whether that value is achieved at some
$(M^\star,\lambda^\star)$. This is attainment (A2), and it is \emph{not} implied by
boundedness: \citet[Thm.~3(d)]{CifuentesDeyXu2025} exhibit a bounded CPP with zero gap
whose dual optimum is not attained.
\end{itemize}
The Owen allocation is read from a dual point, so it is the second question that matters
for an \emph{exact} core. But the first question already delivers a great deal: because a
fixed-multiplier Lagrangian is a coalition-wise upper bound on $\vmip$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:upper}), \emph{any} dual-feasible $(M,\lambda)$ --- optimal or not ---
yields an allocation that is stable, and a $\varepsilon$-optimal $(M,\lambda)$ yields one
that is stable and efficient up to $\varepsilon$. Since \citet[Thm.~3(b)]{CifuentesDeyXu2025}
construct a closed-form $\varepsilon$-optimal dual solution for every $\varepsilon>0$, the
$\varepsilon$-core is available unconditionally, with no appeal to attainment. Everything
below concerns only the exact-core upgrade.

\subsection{The primal Slater obstruction (why the earlier claim fails)}

A sufficient condition for dual attainment is a \emph{primal} Slater point of the CPP
\eqref{eq:cpp}: a feasible lifted matrix in the interior of the completely positive cone.
The earlier draft proposed to manufacture one from bidirectional trade. The following fact
shows no such construction can succeed once binaries are present, independently of the
trade structure.

\begin{proposition}[Burer-type lifts have no primal interior]
\label{prop:no-interior}
Let the lifted feasible set carry at least one binary identity $z=z^2$, written at the
lifted level as $Z=z$ for the corresponding diagonal entry of $Y$ (Step~2). Then $Y$ is
singular at every feasible point: $Y$ can never lie in the interior of the completely
positive cone, so \eqref{eq:cpp} has no primal Slater point.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Consider the principal block of $Y$ on the augmented coordinates $[1,z,s]$ for a single
binary $z$ with its complementing slack $s=1-z$. Feasibility imposes $z+s=1$, the RLT
diagonal $Z+2(zs)+S=1$, and the binary identity $Z=z$. Computing the Schur complement of
the leading $1$ entry gives, after substituting these equalities, the $2\times2$ matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix} Z-z^2 & (zs)-zs\\ (zs)-zs & S-s^2\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix} z-z^2 & (zs)-z+z^2\\ (zs)-z+z^2 & z-z^2-2(zs)\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose determinant, as a function of the free off-diagonal lift $zs$, equals $-(zs)^2\le0$,
with maximum $0$ at $zs=0$. Hence the Schur complement is never positive definite, so $Y$
is never positive definite; being singular, $Y$ lies on the boundary of the positive
semidefinite cone and a fortiori on the boundary of $\CP\subseteq\PSD$. No feasible $Y$ is
interior.
\end{proof}

This is not special to any instance; it is the essence of the binary lift. The identity
$z=z^2$ forces $[1,z]$ to be perfectly correlated in the moment matrix, dropping its rank.
\citet[\S4.1]{BomzeGabl2023} state the same fact as common knowledge: ``for reformulations
based on [Burer's theorem], the most important type of reformulations, it is well known
that the feasible set never has interior.''

\paragraph{The reduction step does not rescue it.}
One might hope to recover a primal Slater point by Burer's \emph{reduction step}
\citep[][\S3]{Burer2012}, which lowers the dimension of the set-copositive constraint and,
in the single-equality case $\{x:e^\top x=1,x\ge0\}$, does produce a strictly feasible
lifted point. But \citet[Prop.~16]{BomzeGabl2023} prove that the reduced reformulation has
a primal Slater point \emph{if and only if there is a single equality constraint}
(formally, $a_i=b_i\alpha$ for all $i$). The community CPP has many equalities --- the
clearing rows across carriers and periods, the reserve and peak rows, and one binary
identity per commitment variable --- so the reduction step leaves it without a primal
interior. Consequently a primal Slater point is unavailable to us by any of these routes,
and the earlier ``bidirectional trade $\Rightarrow$ primal interior'' claim is withdrawn.

\subsection{What survives without attainment: the boundary-loss reading}

That primal Slater fails does not, by itself, defeat the exact core: a primal interior is
\emph{sufficient}, not necessary, for attainment. \citet[\S4.1]{BomzeGabl2023} show that
dropping the dual-attainment requirement costs only \emph{boundary} points of the feasible
set described by the conic reformulation; if the feasible set is not connected merely
through boundary points --- e.g.\ if the relevant constraint sets share a point in their
relative interiors --- one does not need attainment for the value identity. The upshot for
us is precise: the value identity $\val(\mathrm{COP})=\vmip(N)$ holds regardless (so
efficiency is exact in the limit), and only the existence of an \emph{attained} optimizer
is in question. When an attained optimizer exists, the exact core follows
(Theorem~\ref{thm:liu}(i)); when it does not, the dual is approached but not achieved, and
the construction yields the $\varepsilon$-core (Theorem~\ref{thm:liu}(ii)) but not, in
general, a single exact allocation. Attainment is thus an \emph{instance-dependent} open
condition, not one we can certify from community structure.

\subsection{The computational mirror: cutting planes terminate iff attainment holds}

The theoretical dichotomy has an exact algorithmic counterpart, which is how
\citet{GuoEtAl} actually compute copositive prices. Since no solver handles copositive
programs directly, one solves the dual COP by an \emph{outer-approximation} cutting-plane
method: start from the master problem with the conic constraint $\Omega\in\COP$ removed
(an outer relaxation), solve it as a (mixed-integer) linear program, and test the
incumbent $\bar\Omega$ for copositivity via a separation oracle (an MILP using
Anstreicher's certificate); if $\bar\Omega$ is not copositive, add the cut
$\bar z^\top\Omega\bar z\ge0$ and repeat. The dual multipliers --- the prices, and for us
the Owen weights --- are read directly from the master LP's optimal dual at termination,
since by then the conic constraint has been replaced by finitely many linear cuts.

The decisive point is the termination clause: the method \citet{GuoEtAl} use
``exactly solves the COP \emph{when it terminates}.'' Termination means the master optimum
$\bar\Omega$ exists and is copositive --- that is, the dual optimum is \emph{attained} on
that instance. The copositive cone is not polyhedral, so finite termination is not
guaranteed; on an instance where attainment fails (the \citet{CifuentesDeyXu2025}
phenomenon), the algorithm does not terminate, adding cuts indefinitely while the master
value converges to $\vmip(N)$ but the iterates $\bar\Omega_k$ do not converge. Thus the
algorithm realizes the same dichotomy as the theory:
\begin{center}
\emph{cutting plane terminates} $\iff$ \emph{dual attainment holds} $\implies$ \emph{exact
core};\\
otherwise the iterates give $\varepsilon_k$-optimal duals with $\varepsilon_k\to0$, i.e.\
a sequence of $\varepsilon_k$-cores.
\end{center}
This is exactly the position \citet{GuoEtAl} occupy: they do not establish attainment;
their mechanism returns exact prices on instances where the algorithm terminates, and
their individual-rationality guarantee is itself only ``for all but at most $|T|+1$''
generators via a Shapley--Folkman argument, with the system operator absorbing the
residual. Our cooperative analogue inherits the same boundary: an exact core exactly when
the dual is attained (equivalently, when the cutting-plane solver terminates), and an
$\varepsilon$-core --- with $\varepsilon$ controlled by the achieved dual suboptimality
--- always.

\subsection{Honest statement of scope}

The corrected picture is therefore the following, and it is more modest than the earlier
draft but defensible.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{Unconditional.} Value identity $\val(\mathrm{COP})=\vmip(N)$ (boundedness,
\citealp[Thm.~3(a)]{CifuentesDeyXu2025}); hence an $\varepsilon$-core for every
$\varepsilon>0$ via the closed-form $\varepsilon$-optimal dual
\citep[Thm.~3(b)]{CifuentesDeyXu2025}, computed in finite time at any cutting-plane
iterate.
\item \emph{Conditional on attainment (A2).} An exact core
(Theorem~\ref{thm:liu}(i)). Attainment is not implied by boundedness
\citep[Thm.~3(d)]{CifuentesDeyXu2025}, is \emph{not} obtainable from a primal Slater point
(Proposition~\ref{prop:no-interior}; \citealp[Prop.~16]{BomzeGabl2023}), and is equivalent
to finite termination of the copositive cutting-plane solver. We leave it as an
instance-dependent open condition (Open point~\ref{q:attain}).
\end{itemize}
We regard isolating attainment as the precise hypothesis --- separating it cleanly from
the gap, showing the primal-Slater route is structurally closed, and tying it to algorithm
termination --- as the honest contribution here, rather than the withdrawn claim that
community structure makes the exact core automatic. The $\varepsilon$-core, which is what
the method actually computes in finite time, holds with no such hypothesis.

\section{Summary}

The construction dualizes the joint cone \emph{together with} the squared coupling;
the $(\mathrm{c},\mathrm{c})$ cross-block term then cancels rather than being forced
(Remark~\ref{rem:twomech}), and at any fixed dual-feasible multiplier the Lagrangian
is a block-separable \emph{classical} linear production game (Lemma~\ref{lem:vanish})
whose core is nonempty by Owen's theorem (Proposition~\ref{prop:balanced}) and which
upper-bounds $\vmip$ coalition-wise (Corollary~\ref{cor:upper}). No per-player cone is
needed: the off-diagonal blocks are tamed by the coefficient condition and the diagonal
blocks by the affine RLT identities, so the inner game is polyhedral and the redundant
per-player completely positive cones of an earlier version are dropped
(Remark~\ref{rem:no-perplayer-cone}), leaving a \emph{single} strong-duality hypothesis
and a smaller dualized program (Open point~\ref{q:comp}).

The status of the central claim is now precise, and weaker than an earlier draft
asserted. \emph{Strong duality (zero gap) is automatic} from boundedness
\citep{CifuentesDeyXu2025}, so the construction \emph{unconditionally} yields a stable
allocation whose budget exceeds $\vmip(N)$ by at most an arbitrarily small $\varepsilon$
--- an $\varepsilon$-core (Theorem~\ref{thm:liu}(ii)). An \emph{exact} core requires more:
that the copositive dual optimum be \emph{attained} (Theorem~\ref{thm:liu}(i)), which ---
crucially --- does \emph{not} follow from boundedness
\citep[Thm.~3(d)]{CifuentesDeyXu2025} and must be argued from model structure. At an
attained optimum the LPG core is stable and exactly efficient for $\Gamma^{\mathrm{MIP}}$;
a generic multiplier gives only a convex-hull over-estimate (Remark~\ref{rem:Mcone},
Remark~\ref{rem:which-part}).

A companion identity (Section~\ref{sec:chp}) places convex hull pricing within the same
machinery: lifting \emph{only} the per-player feasible sets to their completely positive
cones, with the linear balance kept and the squared coupling and joint cone dropped,
yields exactly convex hull pricing --- the community-balance LP with each $\feas_i$
replaced by $\conv(\feas_i)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:chp}). This is the
per-player-decoupled corner of the construction, the way-station the dual minimization
would otherwise pass through, and it coincides with the convex-hull-pricing allocation of
the companion manuscript; the two constructions agree exactly when the integrality gap
$\vchp(N)-\vmip(N)$ vanishes.

The structural requirement on the coupling is also sharp: the sub-game is a classical LPG
\emph{only} because the community balance is homogeneous (right-hand side $0$). A nonzero
aggregate would add a coalition-independent constant $\lambda\lVert d\rVert^{2}$ to every
coalition value and break the production-game structure (Remark~\ref{rem:homogeneous}).
Realistic extensions preserve the result iff they are homogeneous, player-separable
couplings $\sum_iA_ix_i=\sum_ib_i$ with $b_i=0$.

Three things are not settled.
\emph{(a) Dual attainment.} This, not the gap, is the genuine hypothesis. Boundedness
gives the gap and the $\varepsilon$-core for free; attainment --- which upgrades to an
exact core --- is not implied by boundedness and is the central open point
(Open point~\ref{q:attain}), shared with existing copositive-pricing work
\citep{GuoEtAl}, which assumes a dual optimum without establishing it. We had hoped to
secure attainment structurally, via a primal interior (Slater) point of the community CPP
supplied by bidirectional trade; that route is \emph{closed}, because Burer-type lifts
have no primal interior once binaries are present (Proposition~\ref{prop:no-interior}), and
the reduction step recovers one only for a single equality constraint
\citep[Prop.~16]{BomzeGabl2023}, which the multi-row community CPP is not. What remains is
honest and still structured:
\[
\text{boundedness}\ \Longrightarrow\ \underbrace{\val(\mathrm{COP})=\vmip(N)}_{\text{gap, unconditional}}\
\Longrightarrow\ \varepsilon\text{-core (unconditional)};\qquad
\underbrace{\text{dual attainment}}_{\equiv\ \text{cutting-plane terminates}}\
\Longrightarrow\ \text{\emph{exact} core},
\]
with attainment equivalent to finite termination of the copositive cutting-plane solver
(Section~\ref{sec:slater-structural}) and left as an instance-dependent open condition.
This is a more modest claim than ``replace Bondareva--Shapley'': the $\varepsilon$-core is
unconditional, and the exact core is conditional on a hypothesis we isolate cleanly but do
not discharge.
\emph{(b) Computation.} Computing even the $\varepsilon$-optimal multiplier is
\textsf{NP}-hard, and tractable relaxations drop the guarantee to their own
convex-hull-like level (Open point~\ref{q:comp}). Removing the per-player cones shrinks
the dualized program (one joint copositive constraint, not $n+1$), but does not make
computation cheap.
\emph{(c) Strength beyond the convex-hull fact.} Whether the construction strictly
exceeds ``balanced convex hull $\Rightarrow$ nonempty integer core'' hinges on the
proper-coalition values $\vlr_S(M^{*})$ at the grand optimum; empirically this is governed
by the integrality gap $\vchp(N)-\vmip(N)$, the same quantity that controls whether the
companion convex-hull-pricing identity of Section~\ref{sec:chp} already suffices.

The decisive theoretical question before publication is whether dual attainment can be
characterized for the community CPP by any route other than the (closed) primal-Slater one
--- for instance through a coercivity or closedness property of the copositive dual that
does not require a primal interior --- or whether the $\varepsilon$-core should simply be
adopted as the headline result, with the exact core stated as conditional on attainment
(equivalently, on solver termination). The decisive empirical step is to measure
$\vchp(N)-\vmip(N)$ on the model instances. Together these settle whether the contribution
is an exact-core result under a checkable attainment condition or, more conservatively, an
unconditional $\varepsilon$-core with an attainment-conditional exact-core upgrade.
