\begin{abstract}
Sector-coupled energy communities create operational synergies by jointly dispatching
electricity, hydrogen and heat assets, but electrolyzer unit commitment makes the
community dispatch non-convex. Stable profit allocation then becomes difficult, since
shadow prices are unavailable and the core of the associated cooperative game may be
empty. We show that the linear production game structure that yields a core allocation under
convex dispatch is not lost but hidden. Partially dualizing the constraints that couple members and writing
the relaxation in Dantzig--Wolfe form recovers it, and the resulting allocation is obtained without evaluating an exponential number of coalitions. Spreading the duality gap over the members yields an efficient allocation in
the weak $\varepsilon$-core of the game, and we show the bound of $\varepsilon$ falls as the community grows. Experiments with up to 60 prosumers confirm
the stability results and the scalability.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Energy community, sector coupling, cooperative game theory, mixed-integer linear programming games
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
 
\IEEEPARstart{S}{ector} coupling, a concept of the integration
of electricity with other energy carriers such as hydrogen and heat, has emerged as a
promising strategy to address the intermittency of renewable energy sources~\cite{ramsebner2021sector}. Local energy communities have recently emerged as an effective organizational structure for implementing sector
coupling at the distribution level~\cite{bovera2022energy}. By jointly operating the energy assets of community participants, communities can integrate electricity, hydrogen, and heat systems and generate significant economic synergies. One of the central challenges in the design of energy communities is the allocation of cooperative economic benefits among participants.
 
A desirable allocation must ensure that no subset of participants can improve their
collective payoff by leaving the community. This property is called \emph{stability},
sometimes \emph{group fairness}, and is formalized by the \textit{core} of a
cooperative game~\cite{shapley1971assignment}. When the dispatch model is a convex program, it can be
obtained using duality. When the members are coupled solely through the balance of each
carrier, whose right-hand side is the sum of their individual positions, the dispatch
game is an instance of Owen's \emph{linear production
game}~(LPG)~\cite{owen1975core,granot1986generalized}, and the shadow prices of those
balances induce a core allocation~\cite{valencia2025stable,karaca2019core}. Several
community-market designs price internal exchanges at these duals, whether or not they
invoke the structure
explicitly~\cite{cornelusse2019community,moret2018energy,sanjab2022tso,sangare2024cooperative},
and a core allocation has been obtained for a heat--electricity community by a
problem-specific argument~\cite{mitridati2021design}.
 
The sector coupling of interest here is power to gas, in which an electrolyzer turns
electricity into hydrogen. How to schedule and bid such a unit has become an active
question in power-system
operation~\cite{raheli2023conic,varela2021modeling,saretta2023electrolyzer,johnsen2025value},
because its operation is discrete, the unit holding on, standby and off states and
paying a start-up charge on returning from off, and because its conversion efficiency
varies with load. Carrying these decisions into the community dispatch makes the game
mixed-integer, so its value function carries no shadow prices and the game need not be
balanced, and an efficient and stable allocation may fail to \emph{exist} rather than
only to be computable. Nor can the core be approached by enumeration, since each of its
exponentially many inequalities is itself a unit-commitment problem. What is required is an allocation that is efficient, stable to within a small and
quantifiable margin, and certified without evaluating exponentially many coalitions.


Duality has also been applied to the same non-convex dispatch in wholesale markets, to
a different end. The LP dual of a unit-commitment problem does not support the
efficient integer schedule as a competitive equilibrium, so side payments are
unavoidable~\cite{o2005efficient}. Convex hull pricing prices the convex hull of the
dispatch--commitment set and minimizes the total uplift~\cite{schiro2015convex}, the
energy prices being the duals of the LP relaxation of a Dantzig--Wolfe
reformulation~\cite{andrianesis2021computation}. Copositive pricing goes further and
represents the discrete market exactly, at the cost of a cone over which optimization
is itself intractable~\cite{guo2026copositive}. In the wholesale market these uplifts settle what an individual generator is owed. In the energy community context, the object is instead a division of the whole value among the members, and whether such a division would hold against a group that could leave has not been asked.

Cooperative games whose characteristic function is an integer program have been studied
outside the energy literature. Their core may be empty, so it is efficiency that is
given up rather than stability, and the \emph{optimal profit allocation problem} asks
for the stable allocation that recovers as much of the grand-coalition value as
possible~\cite{caprara2010new}. Duality produces such an allocation without evaluating
every coalition. A linear programming relaxation of the characteristic function yields
one~\cite{deng2008algorithmic}, and a Lagrangian relaxation yields a tighter
one~\cite{liu2016computing}.

Three limitations follow. First, the relaxed game may itself have an empty core, in
which case there is no allocation of it to start from and part of the grand-coalition
value cannot be handed out at all. Second, handing out what is left means returning to
the coalition inequalities one at a time~\cite{drechsel2010computing}, the exact method
the relaxation was meant to replace. In an energy community that has been done only for
a linear dispatch~\cite{fioriti2024fair}, where evaluating a coalition is a linear
program rather than a unit-commitment problem. Third, the stability given up is
computed for the instance at hand and for no other, so nothing is known about how it
behaves as the community grows. 

In this paper, we partially dualize the constraints that couple community members, as \cite{liu2016computing}
dualizes those of a general integer programming game, and write the resulting
relaxation through a Dantzig--Wolfe Reformulation which serves similar convexification trick used in non-convex pricing~\cite{schiro2015convex,andrianesis2021computation}. In the community setting, the partial convexification reveals a linear production game, whose
dual multipliers can construct \emph{allocation} rather than energy prices. The structure a convex
community dispatch offers is therefore recovered, at the price of the duality gap the
convexification opens. We bound that gap in turn, which quantifies the stability the
allocation gives up.

Peer-to-peer designs, in which the members contract bilaterally and no manager
schedules the pooled assets~\cite{sousa2019peer}, are an active line of work
alongside the community-based one. What a member earns there is settled by the
contracts themselves: by a Nash equilibrium where the
members negotiate strategically~\cite{le2020peer,belgioioso2022operationally}, by the multipliers that consensus and ADMM
schemes exchange over reciprocal
trades~\cite{zhou2023fully,zare2024designing}, and the
multi-energy setting has been reached along the same route~\cite{wang2022peer}.
We do not take that route, for two reasons. The reference point in each of these
designs is what a member, or a pair of them, forgoes by not trading, whereas the
question here is what an arbitrary group of members could secure on its own,
which only the coalitional values answer. And the efficiency that the
distributed schemes inherit from the centralized problem rests on its convexity,
which the commitment decisions above remove. We therefore keep the
community-based structure and pose the allocation as a cooperative game.
 
The main contributions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item Allocation in energy communities has been studied almost entirely under convex
dispatch. We carry that construction to a non-convex setting,
formulating a sector-coupled community as a mixed-integer cooperative game
$\Gamma^{\mathrm{MIP}}$ over electricity, hydrogen and heat, whose members are coupled
through the three channels.

\item Dualizing those channels leaves a sub-game that is again a linear production
game (Lemma~\ref{lem:lpg}), which is what survives of the convex construction. The sub-game core thus exists, and its element is attainable in a more scalable manner, whereas
for a general mixed-integer game that sub-game core may be empty and a point of it is reached
only by searching over coalitions~\cite{liu2016computing}. Spreading the duality gap
over the members gives an efficient allocation whose deviation tolerance we bound by
$O(|T|/n)$, decreasing in community size (Proposition~\ref{prop:eps}). The same bound
holds for the least tolerance of the game itself, which is ordinarily accessible only
through all $2^{|N|}-1$ coalition values (Corollary~\ref{cor:cos}).

\item On communities of up to 60 prosumers with electrolyzer commitment and
piecewise-linear efficiency, the allocation is computed at every size, where row
generation returns no certificate within an hour beyond $n=15$. The tolerance
$\varepsilon^{\mathrm{LR}}$ falls by an order of magnitude between $n=6$ and $n=60$,
and the measured excess stays under it at every size. For a regulator, the residual
incentive to leave a sector-coupled community is thus bounded, known ex ante, and
self-extinguishing as the community grows.
\end{itemize}

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}
 
\subsection{Core and Optimal Profit Allocation Problems}
 
A cooperative game with transferable utility is a pair $\Gamma=(N,v)$ with player set $N=\{1,\dots,n\}$ and characteristic function $v:2^N\to\R$, $v(\emptyset)=0$. An \emph{allocation} $\chi\in\R^n$ is \emph{efficient} if $\sum_{j\in N}\chi_j=v(N)$ and \emph{stable} if $\sum_{j\in S}\chi_j\ge v(S)$ for all $S\subseteq N$. \emph{Core}, denoted by $\mathcal{C}_{v}$, is the set of efficient and stable allocations defined as follows~\cite{shapley1971assignment},
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:core}
\begin{align}
\mathcal{C}(v)=\{\chi\in\R^n: \textstyle\sum_{j\in N}\chi_j=v(N), \\\sum_{j\in S}\chi_j\ge v(S)\, \forall S \subseteq N\}.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
When $v$ is the value function of a mixed-integer program (MIP), deciding membership of any allocation $\chi$ is coNP-complete, and $\mathcal{C}(v)$ can be even empty, and testing non-emptiness of the core is also coNP-complete~\cite{deng2008algorithmic}. Therefore, we consider the \emph{optimal partial allocation problem (OPAP)}~\cite{caprara2010new} defined as follows:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\chi\in\R^n}\Big\{\textstyle\sum_{j\in N}\chi_j:\ \sum_{j\in S}\chi_j\ge v(S)\ \forall S \subseteq N\Big\}.
\label{eq:opap}
\end{equation}
\emph{OPAP}~\eqref{eq:opap} is well-defined if $v(S)<+\infty$ for any $S\subseteq N$, even if $\mathcal{C}(v)=\emptyset$.
Let $\alpha^{*}$ be the optimal value of the OPAP~\eqref{eq:opap}. 
Any feasible $\chi$ of \eqref{eq:opap} is stable, $\alpha^{*}\ge v(N)$, and the excess
\begin{equation}
\omega^{*}:=\alpha^{*}-v(N)
\label{eq:excess}
\end{equation}
is the minimum external subsidy that makes the grand coalition stable, called as \emph{cost of stability}~\cite{bachrach2009cost}. The core is nonempty if and only if $\omega^{*}=0$.
 
The \emph{weak $\varepsilon$-core} $\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(v)$~\cite{shapley1971assignment}
relaxes the stability requirement of the core,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(v)=\Big\{\chi:\ \textstyle\sum_{j\in N}\chi_j=v(N),\
\sum_{j\in S}\chi_j\ge v(S)-\varepsilon|S|\ \forall S\Big\},
\label{eq:weakeps}
\end{equation}
so that no coalition can improve by more than $\varepsilon$ per member by deviating.
We accordingly read $\varepsilon$ as a \emph{deviation tolerance}, and write
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon^{*}:=\min\{\varepsilon\ge0:\ \mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(v)\neq\emptyset\}
\label{eq:eps_star}
\end{equation}
for the least tolerance at which the relaxed core is nonempty.

Feasible solutions of \eqref{eq:opap} and elements of \eqref{eq:weakeps}
correspond to each other. If $\chi$ is feasible for \eqref{eq:opap} with
$\ssum_{j\in N}\chi_j=v(N)+\omega$, then $\chi_j-\omega/n$ is efficient and belongs to
$\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(v)$ with $\varepsilon=\omega/n$; conversely, if
$\chi\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(v)$, then $\chi_j+\varepsilon$ is feasible for
\eqref{eq:opap} with objective value $v(N)+n\varepsilon$. Evaluating the first
direction at $\omega^{*}$ and the second at $\varepsilon^{*}$ gives
$\varepsilon^{*}\le\omega^{*}/n$ and $\omega^{*}\le n\varepsilon^{*}$, hence
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon^{*}=\omega^{*}/n .
\label{eq:eps_omega}
\end{equation}
Therefore, the two quantities can be interpreted as the same measure of instability in different units. We state our results in terms of $\varepsilon$ of \emph{weak $\epsilon$-core}, as the allocation we propose is characterized by the weak $\varepsilon$-core it belongs to.

Both solution concepts are unchanged by a shift of the characteristic
function that is additive over the members. Let $\kappa\in\R^{n}$ and
\begin{equation}
v^{0}(S):=v(S)-\ssum_{j\in S}\kappa_j,\qquad S\subseteq N .
\label{eq:normgame}
\end{equation}
\begin{remark}[Normalization]
\label{rmk:norm}
$\chi\in\mathcal{C}(v)$ if and only if $\chi-\kappa\in\mathcal{C}(v^{0})$,
and $\chi\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(v)$ if and only if
$\chi-\kappa\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(v^{0})$ with the same
$\varepsilon$. Consequently $\alpha^{*}(v^{0})=\alpha^{*}(v)-\ssum_{j\in
N}\kappa_j$, and $\omega^{*}$ and $\varepsilon^{*}$ coincide for the two
games.
\end{remark}
Every constraint of \eqref{eq:core}, \eqref{eq:opap} and \eqref{eq:weakeps}
has $\ssum_{j\in S}\kappa_j$ on both sides, which gives the equivalences;
the statement on $\alpha^{*}$ follows by taking $S=N$ in the objective of
\eqref{eq:opap}. Taking $\kappa_j=v(\{j\})$ makes $v^{0}(\{j\})=0$, so an
allocation of $v^{0}$ reports what a member gains by joining the coalition.
\subsection{Linear Production Games and the Owen Solution}
A cooperative game whose characteristic function is generated by a linear program of a
particular structure is called a \emph{linear production game} (LPG), which serves as the analytical basis for our study.
\begin{definition}[Linear production game~\cite{owen1975core}]
\label{def:lpg}
$\Gamma=(N,v)$ is a \emph{linear production game} if there exist, for each $j\in N$, an endowment $b_j\in\R^m$, a technology matrix $A_j$ and an objective vector $c_j$, together with an optional common technology $(A_0,c_0)$, such that
\begin{align}
v(S)=\max_{x_j,x_0\ge 0}\ & \textstyle\sum_{j\in S}c_j^{\top}x_j+c_0^{\top}x_0 \label{eq:lpg_obj}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\ & \textstyle\sum_{j\in S}A_jx_j+A_0x_0\le \sum_{j\in S}b_j \quad (\mu). \label{eq:lpg_res}
\end{align}
\end{definition}
 
Coalition $S$ pools the endowments of its members and produces subject to what it has pooled. Let $\mu^{*}$ solve the dual of \eqref{eq:lpg_obj}--\eqref{eq:lpg_res} at $S=N$. The \emph{Owen solution} is
\begin{equation}
\chi_j^{\mathrm{OW}}:=(\mu^{*})^{\top}b_j,\qquad j\in N,
\label{eq:owen}
\end{equation}
and it lies in the core, which implies the LPG has always a nonempty core. The \textit{Owen solution} certifies stability against \emph{all} $2^{|N|}-1$ coalitions from a single grand-coalition solve, and it does so without ever evaluating $v(S)$ for $S\neq N$.

\section{Problem Setting}

\subsection{Modeling Assumptions}


We consider a community of prosumers $N=\{1,\dots,|N|\}$ that pool their resources
---renewable generation, storage, sector-coupling conversion, and demand across the
three energy carriers $K=\{E,G,H\}$ (electricity, hydrogen, and heat)---and interact
with external wholesale markets and the system operator through a single point of
common coupling. Following the community-manager setting standard in this
literature~\cite{cornelusse2019community,moret2018energy,mitridati2021design}, we
address the day-ahead operational planning stage.

\begin{assumption}[Operating environment]
\label{as:env}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] A central community manager schedules all member assets over the horizon
$T$.
\item[(ii)] The community is a price taker in the wholesale and reserve markets.
\item[(iii)] Import prices are no lower than export prices,
$\pi_t^{k,imkt}\ge\pi_t^{k,emkt}$, as retail markups and network charges make
them~\cite{cornelusse2019community}.
\item[(iv)] The schedule is computed against the forecasts without accounting for
their error.
\item[(v)] Preferences are quasi-linear in money, and $\bar u_{jt}^k$ is a
willingness to pay expressed in \euro{} per unit of carrier $k$, so that
payoffs are transferable among members at par.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

Under Assumption~\ref{as:env}, the manager maximizes the joint welfare of
the members and distributes the gains that cooperation creates over what
they could achieve alone. A non-cooperative formulation, such as a
peer-to-peer trading model, as describeda above, addresses a different question which quantifies neither the value
that cooperation creates nor the extent to which non-convex operation harms that value. Incentive compatibility is likewise outside our scope, as it cannot
be attained together with welfare maximization and budget balance, and what is at
issue here is how the synergies among the sector-coupling technologies are shared,
given the data the members report. Price taking is appropriate at the scale of a
single community, and it keeps $\pi_i^{res}$ and $\pi_t^{k,\cdot}$ independent of
$S$, without which a coalition's revenue would not be additive in its members
(Remark~\ref{rmk:nonadditive}). Setting forecast error aside isolates the effect of non-convexity, which is the
subject of this paper, and Remark~\ref{rmk:stoch} indicates how it can be carried
explicitly.

\begin{assumption}[Community as a resource pool]
\label{as:pool}
Every constraint that couples distinct members is a linear (in)equality that is
additive over the members,
\begin{equation}
\ssum_{j\in S}A_j^{(\ell)}x_j+A_0^{(\ell)}x_0\ \le\ \ssum_{j\in S}b_j^{(\ell)},
\quad \ell=1,\cdots,m,
\label{eq:linking}
\end{equation}
where $x_0$ collects the
community-level variables, which appear on the left-hand side only.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{as:pool} places all cooperation in \eqref{eq:linking}. Members bring
their own assets and operate them jointly, exchanging carriers and contributing to
the community-level quantities only through \eqref{eq:linking}. Both of its sides add
up over whoever is present, so a coalition faces the constraints of the grand
coalition with the absent members dropped from every sum. This is the community-based
structure of the consumer-centric market taxonomy~\cite{sousa2019peer}, in which
members supply each other from shared resources and face the external markets as one
entity~\cite{moret2018energy,cornelusse2019community,valencia2025stable}.

Community-owned assets are covered as far as their capacity can be divided. Suppose
each member holds a fixed share of such an asset. The share enters \eqref{eq:linking}
as $b_j^{(\ell)}$ on the right-hand side of the rows that cap the asset's use, so a
coalition may operate the asset up to the sum of the shares its members
hold~\cite{vespermann2020access}. The division reproduces the asset exactly when its
feasible set is convex, which holds for storage but not for a unit with a commitment
state.

\subsection{Community Dispatch Model}
\label{sec:model}
 
Let $x_j$ collect all decision variables of prosumer $j$ over $T$, including its binary commitment variables $y_j$, and let $x_0=\big((r_i^{sym})_{i\in I},p\big)^{\top}\ge 0$ collect the community-level variables: the symmetric reserve capacity sold in each block and the peak net grid import. The dispatch problem of a coalition $S\subseteq N$ is
% NOTE: subequations sub-labels use \alph (a--z). Keep numbered lines <= 26,
% otherwise LaTeX raises "Counter too large". Currently 24.
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:dp}
\begin{align}
&(\mathrm{DP}_S):\ \max\ \sum_{j\in S}\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{t\in T}\Big(\pi_t^{k,emkt}e_{jt}^{k,mkt}-\pi_t^{k,imkt}i_{jt}^{k,mkt} \nonumber\\
&+\bar u_{jt}^kd_{jt}^{k,load}-\bar c_{jt}^kp_{jt}^k\Big)-\ssum_{j\in S}\ssum_{t\in T}c_j^{G,su}z_{jt}^{G,su} \nonumber\\
&+\ \ssum_{i\in I}\pi_i^{res}r_i^{sym}-\delta^{peak}p \label{eq:dp_obj}
\end{align}
subject to the community-level linking constraints
\begin{align}
& \ssum_{j\in S}\big(i_{jt}^{k,com}-e_{jt}^{k,com}\big)=0,\quad \forall t,k  \label{eq:dp_bal}\\
& r_i^{sym}\le\ssum_{j\in S}r_{jt}^{u},\ r_i^{sym}\le\ssum_{j\in S}r_{jt}^{d},\ \forall i\in I,\ t\in T_i \label{eq:dp_res}\\
& \ssum_{j\in S}\big(i_{jt}^{E,mkt}-e_{jt}^{E,mkt}\big)\le p,\quad \forall t  \label{eq:dp_peak}
\end{align}
and, for every $j\in S$ and $t\in T$, to the prosumer-level operational constraints
\begin{align}
& e_{jt}^{E,mkt}\!-i_{jt}^{E,mkt}\!+e_{jt}^{E,com}\!-i_{jt}^{E,com}\!-p_{jt}^{E}\!+d_{jt}^{E,load} \nonumber\\
&\hspace{7.2em}+d_{jt}^{G,sc}+d_{jt}^{H,sc}=0, \label{eq:pb_E}\\
& e_{jt}^{k,mkt}\!-i_{jt}^{k,mkt}\!+e_{jt}^{k,com}\!-i_{jt}^{k,com}\!-p_{jt}^{k}\!+d_{jt}^{k,load}=0,\nonumber\\
&\hspace{7.2em}k\in\{G,H\}, \label{eq:pb_GH}\\
& \underline p_{jt}^k\le p_{jt}^k\le\overline p_{jt}^k,\ \ \underline d_{jt}^{k,load}\le d_{jt}^{k,load}\le\overline d_{jt}^{k,load}, \label{eq:bnd_pd}\\
& 0\le i_{jt}^{k,com}\le\overline i_j^{k,com},\quad
  0\le e_{jt}^{k,com}\le\overline e_j^{k,com}, \nonumber\\
& 0\le i_{jt}^{k,mkt}\le\overline i_j^{k,mkt},\quad
  0\le e_{jt}^{k,mkt}\le\overline e_j^{k,mkt}, \label{eq:bnd_trade}\\
& s_{jt}^k=s_{j,t-1}^k+\nu_j^{k,ch}b_{jt}^{k,ch}-\tfrac{1}{\nu_j^{k,dis}}b_{jt}^{k,dis}, \label{eq:soc}\\
& 0\le s_{jt}^k\le\overline s_j^k,\ \ 0\le b_{jt}^{k,ch}\le\overline b_j^{k,ch},\ \ 0\le b_{jt}^{k,dis}\le\overline b_j^{k,dis}, \label{eq:bnd_sto}\\
& r_{jt}^{u}=r_{jt}^{u,\mathrm{sto}}+r_{jt}^{u,\mathrm{els}}+r_{jt}^{u,\mathrm{hp}}, \label{eq:res_agg_u}\\
& r_{jt}^{d}=r_{jt}^{d,\mathrm{sto}}+r_{jt}^{d,\mathrm{els}}+r_{jt}^{d,\mathrm{hp}}, \label{eq:res_agg_d}\\
& b_{jt}^{E,dis}\!-b_{jt}^{E,ch}\!+r_{jt}^{u,\mathrm{sto}}\le\overline b_j^{E,dis},\ \ r_{jt}^{u,\mathrm{sto}}\le\nu_j^{E,dis}s_{jt}^E, \nonumber\\
& b_{jt}^{E,ch}\!-b_{jt}^{E,dis}\!+r_{jt}^{d,\mathrm{sto}}\le\overline b_j^{E,ch},\ \ r_{jt}^{d,\mathrm{sto}}\le\tfrac{\overline s_j^E-s_{jt}^E}{\nu_j^{E,ch}}, \label{eq:res_sto}
\end{align}
together with the sector-coupling technology constraints. For the electrolyzer, with state indicators $z_{jt}^{G,on},z_{jt}^{G,sb},z_{jt}^{G,off}$, transition indicators $z_{jt}^{G,su},z_{jt}^{G,sd}$, and segment indicators $\hat z_{jlt}$,
\begin{align}
& z_{jt}^{G,on}+z_{jt}^{G,sb}+z_{jt}^{G,off}=1, \label{eq:els_state}\\
& z_{jt}^{G,su}\ge z_{jt}^{G,on}+z_{j,t-1}^{G,off}+z_{j,t-1}^{G,sb}-1, \nonumber\\
& z_{jt}^{G,sd}\ge z_{j,t-1}^{G,on}+z_{jt}^{G,off}+z_{jt}^{G,sb}-1, \label{eq:els_trans}\\
& z_{jt}^{G,su}+z_{jt}^{G,sd}\le 1,\qquad z_{j,t-1}^{G,off}+z_{jt}^{G,sb}\le 1, \label{eq:els_excl}\\
& d_{jt}^{G,sc}=\tilde d_{jt}^{G,sc}+c_G^{sb}z_{jt}^{G,sb}, \label{eq:els_decomp}\\
& \underline D_{jt}^{G}\le d_{jt}^{G,sc}\le\overline D_{jt}^{G}, \label{eq:els_bnd}\\
& \underline d^{G}z_{jt}^{G,on}\le\tilde d_{jt}^{G,sc}-r_{jt}^{u,\mathrm{els}}, \label{eq:els_res_u}\\
& \tilde d_{jt}^{G,sc}+r_{jt}^{d,\mathrm{els}}\le\overline d^{G}z_{jt}^{G,on}, \label{eq:els_res_d}\\
& p_{jt}^G=\ssum_{l\in L}\big(\phi_l^1\hat d_{jlt}+\phi_l^0\hat z_{jlt}\big),\ \ \underline P_l\hat z_{jlt}\le\hat d_{jlt}\le\overline P_l\hat z_{jlt}, \label{eq:els_pwl}\\
& z_{jt}^{G,on}=\ssum_{l\in L}\hat z_{jlt},\quad \tilde d_{jt}^{G,sc}=\ssum_{l\in L}\hat d_{jlt}, \label{eq:els_link}
\end{align}
and, for the heat pump,
\begin{align}
& p_{jt}^H=\nu^{\mathrm{COP}}d_{jt}^{H,sc}, \label{eq:hp_conv}\\
& 0\le d_{jt}^{H,sc}\le\overline d^{H}, \label{eq:hp_bnd}\\
& r_{jt}^{u,\mathrm{hp}}\le d_{jt}^{H,sc},\quad d_{jt}^{H,sc}+r_{jt}^{d,\mathrm{hp}}\le\overline d^{H}, \label{eq:hp_res}\\
& y_j:=\big(z_{jt}^{G,\cdot},\hat z_{jlt}\big)\in\{0,1\}^{n_{y_j}},\quad r_{jt}^{u,\cdot},\,r_{jt}^{d,\cdot}\ge 0. \label{eq:bin}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Here $\underline D_{jt}^{G},\overline D_{jt}^{G}$ abbreviate the commitment-dependent power window of the electrolyzer,
\begin{equation}
\underline D_{jt}^{G}=\underline d^{G}z_{jt}^{G,on}+c_G^{sb}z_{jt}^{G,sb},\quad
\overline D_{jt}^{G}=\overline d^{G}z_{jt}^{G,on}+c_G^{sb}z_{jt}^{G,sb},
\label{eq:window}
\end{equation}
which collapses to $\{0\}$ when the unit is off. Constraints \eqref{eq:els_state}--\eqref{eq:els_link} and \eqref{eq:hp_conv}--\eqref{eq:hp_res} apply only to prosumers owning the corresponding technology; all remaining constraints are carrier-wise and vacuous for assets a prosumer does not own.
 
The objective~\eqref{eq:dp_obj} is the community's social welfare: net external trading revenue, consumption utility net of production and start-up cost, reserve capacity revenue, and the peak-import charge.  
Three types of constraints couple community members. Constraint~\eqref{eq:dp_bal} clears
internal trading of each carrier in each period. Constraint~\eqref{eq:dp_res}
requires the capacity sold in a block to be deliverable in both directions in
every period of that block, upward by reducing net consumption and downward by
increasing it, so within each block the scarcest period and direction bind.
Constraint~\eqref{eq:dp_peak} charges the community for its largest net grid
import over the horizon, which is the peak-shaving incentive it faces. 

The reserve is a symmetric capacity product sold by the block. The horizon is
partitioned into blocks $T=\bigcup_{i\in I}T_i$, and within block $i$ the community
sells a single quantity $r_i^{sym}$ at the exogenous capacity price $\pi_i^{res}$.
Remuneration is for capacity only, since the activation of such products oscillates
around the setpoint and its net energy is
negligible~\cite{saretta2023electrolyzer,johnsen2025value}.

The remaining constraints govern the operation of each member's own assets.
Constraints~\eqref{eq:pb_E}--\eqref{eq:pb_GH} balance each carrier at the member
level, the conversion inputs $d_{jt}^{G,sc}$ and $d_{jt}^{H,sc}$ entering
\eqref{eq:pb_E} as electricity loads and the converted quantities $p_{jt}^{G}$ and
$p_{jt}^{H}$ entering \eqref{eq:pb_GH} as production. Constraint~\eqref{eq:bnd_pd} bounds production and load, \eqref{eq:bnd_trade} bounds
each of the four trades of prosumer $j$, and \eqref{eq:soc}--\eqref{eq:bnd_sto}
propagate the state of charge of each carrier's storage and bound it together with the
charge and discharge power. Constraints~\eqref{eq:soc}--\eqref{eq:bnd_sto} propagate
the state of charge of each carrier's storage and bound it together with the charge
and discharge power. Constraints~\eqref{eq:res_agg_u}--\eqref{eq:res_sto} sum a
member's reserve contribution over its flexible assets and limit the storage part to
the headroom left by its charge and discharge schedule in each direction.

The electrolyzer is modeled following~\cite{raheli2023conic}.
Constraints~\eqref{eq:els_state}--\eqref{eq:els_excl} track its state among on,
standby and off, and charge $c_j^{G,su}$ whenever the unit returns from off.
Constraint~\eqref{eq:els_decomp} separates the standby draw $c_G^{sb}$, which avoids
a cold restart~\cite{varela2021modeling}, from the productive input $\tilde
d_{jt}^{G,sc}$, and \eqref{eq:els_bnd} applies the window~\eqref{eq:window} to the
total draw. Constraint~\eqref{eq:els_res_u}--\eqref{eq:els_res_d} admits a reserve contribution only while
the unit is on~\cite{saretta2023electrolyzer}. The power-to-hydrogen curve is
concave, and we approximate it by a piecewise-linear function in
\eqref{eq:els_pwl}--\eqref{eq:els_link}, with $\hat z_{jlt}$ selecting the active
segment, so that $(\mathrm{DP}_S)$ remains a mixed-integer linear program. The heat
pump converts electricity to heat at a constant coefficient of performance within a
rated capacity, \eqref{eq:hp_conv}--\eqref{eq:hp_res}, with no commitment state and
no start-up charge.

We set the community bounds at $\overline i_j^{k,com},\overline e_j^{k,com}\ge R_j^{k}$
and the market bounds a further $R_j^{k}$ above them,
\begin{equation}
\overline i_j^{k,mkt}\ \ge\ \overline i_j^{k,com}+R_j^{k},\qquad
\overline e_j^{k,mkt}\ \ge\ \overline e_j^{k,com}+R_j^{k},
\label{eq:bnd_size}
\end{equation}
where $R_j^{k}$ is the largest net consumption of carrier $k$ that the assets of
prosumer $j$ can have. By Assumption~\ref{as:env}(iii), some optimal dispatch of every coalition has no member
buying a carrier inside the community and selling it outside in the same period, so the
bounds keep $\mathcal{X}_j^{\mathrm{MIP}}$ bounded and leave every coalition value
unchanged.

It is convenient to write \eqref{eq:dp} compactly. Collecting the constraints
\eqref{eq:pb_E}--\eqref{eq:bin} of prosumer $j$ gives the a \emph{mixed-integer linear representable} (bounded) operational region
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{X}_j^{\mathrm{MIP}}=\big\{(x_j,y_j)\in\R^{n_{x_j}}\times\{0,1\}^{n_{y_j}}:\ T_jx_j+W_jy_j\le h_j\big\},
\label{eq:Xmip}
\end{equation}
we write $f_j(x_j,y_j)$ for the terms of \eqref{eq:dp_obj} that belong to prosumer
$j$, which are linear, and $c_0^{\top}x_0$ with
$c_0=\big((\pi_i^{res})_{i\in I},-\delta^{peak}\big)^{\top}$ for the community-level
terms. Stacking \eqref{eq:dp_bal}--\eqref{eq:dp_peak}, each equality written as a pair of
inequalities, gives $\ssum_{j\in S}A_jx_j+A_0x_0\le\ssum_{j\in S}b_j$, the form of
Assumption~\ref{as:pool}, with $m:=(2|K|+3)|T|$ rows and common technology
$(A_0,c_0)$. Here $b_j=0$ for every $j$ and
every row. We refer to these objects rather than to the individual constraints from
here on.

The cooperative game induced by \eqref{eq:dp} takes as the value of a
coalition the welfare surplus its members gain by operating their assets
jointly rather than separately, as is standard in community-based
designs~\cite{mitridati2021design}.
\begin{definition}[Mixed-integer energy community game]
\label{def:game}
Let $\vdp{S}$ be the optimal value of the problem~\eqref{eq:dp} and
$\kappa_j:=\vdp{\{j\}}$. The \emph{mixed-integer energy community game} is
$\Gamma^{\mathrm{MIP}}=(N,v)$ with
\begin{equation}
v(S):=\vdp{S}-\ssum_{j\in S}\kappa_j,\qquad v(\emptyset)=0 .
\label{eq:v0}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Evaluating $v(\cdot)$ once is already NP-hard~\cite{bendotti2019complexity},
\eqref{eq:core} involves $2^{|N|}-1$ such evaluations, and row generation avoids that
enumeration only by selecting a coalition on top of the same problem
(Section~\ref{sec:complexity}). $\Gamma^{\mathrm{MIP}}$ also need not be balanced, so
the core may be empty, in which case no efficient allocation is stable. The next section constructs an allocation in the weak $\varepsilon$-core~\eqref{eq:weakeps} from $\vdp{N}$ and $\{\kappa_j\}_{j\in N}$, without evaluating $\vdp{S}$ for any
coalition of two or more members.

\section{Duality-Based Profit Allocation}
\label{sec:duality}
 
Without the binaries of \eqref{eq:els_state}--\eqref{eq:els_link}, $(\mathrm{DP}_S)$
would be a linear program of the form of Definition~\ref{def:lpg}, and the multipliers
of \eqref{eq:dp_bal}--\eqref{eq:dp_peak} would induce a core allocation, the
construction on which several community designs
rest~\cite{mitridati2021design,sanjab2022tso,valencia2025stable}. The binaries remove
both the multipliers and the guarantee, and we recover what is left of the
construction by relaxation.

We dualize the linking constraints \eqref{eq:dp_bal}--\eqref{eq:dp_peak}, leaving the
members' own constraints intact. Section~\ref{sec:lr} shows that the relaxed game is a
linear production game, and obtains from it an allocation of $\Gamma^{\mathrm{MIP}}$
that is stable but relaxes efficiency by the duality gap. Section~\ref{sec:eps} bounds
that gap, and with it the deviation tolerance the allocation requires.
 
\subsection{Lagrangian Relaxation Based Profit Allocation}
\label{sec:lr}
 
Write the objective~\eqref{eq:dp_obj} as $\sum_{j\in S}f_j(x_j,y_j)+c_0^{\top}x_0$ and the linking constraints. Dualizing the latter with a multiplier $\theta\ge 0$ gives, for every coalition $S$,
\begin{align}
&\vlr(S;\theta)=\max\Big\{\textstyle\sum_{j\in S}f_j(x_j,y_j)+c_0^{\top}x_0 \nonumber \\
&-\theta^{\top}\big(\textstyle\sum_{j\in S}A_jx_j+A_0x_0-\sum_{j\in S}b_j\big)\\ &:\ (x_j,y_j)\in\mathcal{X}_j^{\mathrm{MIP}}\ \forall j\in S,\ x_0\ge 0\Big\}.
\label{eq:vlr}
\end{align}
Once the linking constraints are dualized, the only restriction left on $x_0$ is
$x_0\ge0$, and its contribution to \eqref{eq:vlr} is
$\max_{x_0\ge0}(c_0-A_0^{\top}\theta)^{\top}x_0$. This is $+\infty$ unless
$A_0^{\top}\theta\ge c_0$, and zero otherwise, so we restrict $\theta$ to
\begin{equation}
\Theta:=\big\{\theta\ge0:\ A_0^{\top}\theta\ge c_0\big\},
\label{eq:Theta}
\end{equation}
the dual feasibility condition for the common technology of Definition~\ref{def:lpg}.
The community-level terms then drop out, and \eqref{eq:vlr} separates across
prosumers:
\begin{equation}
\vlr(S;\theta)=\textstyle\sum_{j\in S}\vlr_j(\theta),
\label{eq:vlr_sep}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\vlr_j(\theta):=\theta^{\top}b_j+\max_{(x_j,y_j)\in\mathcal{X}_j^{\mathrm{MIP}}}\big\{f_j(x_j,y_j)-\theta^{\top}A_jx_j\big\}.
\label{eq:vlr_j}
\end{equation}
Here we omit the term $b_{j}$ since $b_{j}=0\;\forall j\in N$.
For each $\theta\in\Theta$, weak duality gives
\begin{equation}
\vlr(S;\theta)\ \ge\ \vdp{S},\qquad \forall S\subseteq N,\ \forall\theta\in\Theta,
\label{eq:weakdual}
\end{equation}
and the tightest of these bounds defines the \emph{Lagrangian sub-game}
$\Gamma^{\mathrm{LR}}=(N,\bar v^{\mathrm{LR}})$ with
$\bar v^{\mathrm{LR}}(S):=\min_{\theta\in\Theta}\vlr(S;\theta)$. The sub-game is still 
assembled from the mixed-integer sets $\mathcal{X}_j^{\mathrm{MIP}}$, but it has an equivalent \emph{linear production game} structure. We reveal it through the \emph{Dantzig-Wolfe} reformulation.

Let $Q_j$ index the extreme points of $\conv(\mathcal{X}_j^{\mathrm{MIP}})$, which is a bounded polyhedron, and write
$w_j^q:=f_j(x_j^q,y_j^q)$ and $a_j^q:=A_jx_j^q$ for the profit and the linking-row
contribution of pattern $q\in Q_j$. Let $(\mathrm{DWR}_S)$ denote the Dantzig--Wolfe
relaxed master problem by relaxing $\lambda_{j}^{q}\in \{0,1\}$ to $\lambda_{j}^{q}\ge 0$,
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:dwr}
\begin{align}
&(\mathrm{DWR}_S):\ \max\  \ssum_{j\in S}\ssum_{q\in Q_j}w_j^q\lambda_j^q+c_0^{\top}x_0 \label{eq:dwr_obj}\\
&\mathrm{s.t.}\  \ssum_{j\in S}\ssum_{q\in Q_j}a_j^q\lambda_j^q+A_0x_0\le\ssum_{j\in S}b_j\ \ (\theta) \label{eq:dwr_link}\\
& \ssum_{q\in Q_j}\lambda_j^q=1,\quad \forall j\in S \quad (\sigma_j) \label{eq:dwr_conv}\\
& \lambda_j^q\ge 0,\quad x_0\ge 0. \label{eq:dwr_nn}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
\begin{lemma}[Linear production structure of the sub-game]
\label{lem:lpg}
Under Assumption~\ref{as:pool}, the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $\bar v^{\mathrm{LR}}(S)=\mathrm{val}(\mathrm{DWR}_S)$ for every
$S\subseteq N$, so $\Gamma^{\mathrm{LR}}$ is the linear production game generated by
$(\mathrm{DWR}_S)$ in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:lpg}, in which prosumer $j$
endows the linking rows~\eqref{eq:dwr_link} with $b_j$ and its own convexity
row~\eqref{eq:dwr_conv} with one unit.
\item[(b)] Let $(\theta^{*},\sigma^{*})$ be an optimal dual solution of
$(\mathrm{DWR}_N)$. Then $\theta^{*}\in\Theta$, and the Owen solution~\eqref{eq:owen}
of $\Gamma^{\mathrm{LR}}$ is $(\theta^{*})^{\top}b_j+\sigma_j^{*}=\vlr_j(\theta^{*})$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
For (a), the Lagrangian dual value of a mixed-integer program equals the value of its
Dantzig--Wolfe master over $\conv(\mathcal{X}_j^{\mathrm{MIP}})$
\cite{desrosiers2024branch}. Comparing \eqref{eq:dwr_link}--\eqref{eq:dwr_conv} with
\eqref{eq:lpg_res} identifies the endowments, both sides of the linking rows being
additive over $S$ by Assumption~\ref{as:pool} and the convexity row of $j$ being
present precisely when $j\in S$. Part (b) follows because the pricing problem of
$(\mathrm{DWR}_N)$ at $\theta^{*}$ is exactly \eqref{eq:vlr_j}, so
$\sigma_j^{*}$ attains the maximum in it.
\end{IEEEproof}

Every row of \eqref{eq:dp_bal}--\eqref{eq:dp_peak} has a zero right-hand
side, so $b_j=0$ and the Owen solution~\eqref{eq:owen} of
$\Gamma^{\mathrm{LR}}$ reduces to $\sigma_j^{*}$. Measuring it against the
stand-alone value of $j$ gives the allocation
\begin{equation}
\chi_j^{\mathrm{OW}}:=\sigma_j^{*}-\kappa_j,\qquad j\in N.
\label{eq:chi_ow}
\end{equation}
 
The linear production game of Lemma~\ref{lem:lpg} reads as resource pooling. What
prosumer $j$ brings is the set of schedules its own assets can run, represented in
$(\mathrm{DWR}_S)$ by the patterns $Q_j$ and by the single unit of \eqref{eq:dwr_conv}
that entitles a coalition to use them. That unit is divisible, the weights
$\lambda_j^q$ being continuous rather than binary, so a coalition may run a convex
combination of the schedules of $j$ where $(\mathrm{DP}_S)$ admits only one of them. We call such a point a \emph{fractional
schedule}, its units being committed fractionally. Since
$\sigma_j^{*}=\max_{q\in Q_j}\{w_j^q-(\theta^{*})^{\top}a_j^q\}$ by \eqref{eq:vlr_j},
the Owen solution credits each member with the most its
own assets can earn at the community's internal prices $\theta^{*}$.
 
\begin{proposition}[Stability]
\label{prop:opap}
Let $\theta^{*}$ be an optimal dual solution of $(\mathrm{DWR}_N)$ and
$\chi^{\mathrm{OW}}$ the Owen solution of $\Gamma^{\mathrm{LR}}$. Then
$\ssum_{j\in S}\chi_j^{\mathrm{OW}}\ge v(S)$ for every $S\subseteq N$, so
$\chi^{\mathrm{OW}}$ is feasible for $(\mathrm{OPAP})$ of $\Gamma^{\mathrm{MIP}}$, and
its excess is the duality gap,
\begin{equation}
\omega^{\mathrm{LR}}:=\ssum_{j\in N}\chi_j^{\mathrm{OW}}-v(N)\ \ge\ 0 .
\label{eq:omega_lr}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
By \eqref{eq:vlr_sep} and \eqref{eq:chi_ow},
$\ssum_{j\in S}\chi_j^{\mathrm{OW}}=\vlr(S;\theta^{*})-\ssum_{j\in
S}\kappa_j$ for every $S$, and $\vlr(S;\theta^{*})\ge\vdp{S}$ by
\eqref{eq:weakdual} since $\theta^{*}\in\Theta$, so the left-hand side is
at least $v(S)$. Taking $S=N$ gives \eqref{eq:omega_lr}, whose right-hand
side is $\mathrm{val}(\mathrm{DWR}_N)-\vdp{N}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:lpg}.
\end{IEEEproof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:opap} makes $\chi^{\mathrm{OW}}$ a near-optimal solution of
$(\mathrm{OPAP})$, feasible and exceeding $v(N)$ by the duality gap
$\omega^{\mathrm{LR}}$. It is moreover the Owen solution of $\Gamma^{\mathrm{LR}}$,
read from the dual of $(\mathrm{DWR}_N)$, so it is obtained without solving an
$(\mathrm{OPAP})$ at all. Both rest on the linear production structure of
Lemma~\ref{lem:lpg}, which a general mixed-integer game does not have. There the
sub-game core may be empty and, when it is not, yields an element only through a
further $(\mathrm{OPAP})$ for the sub-game~\cite{liu2016computing}.
Section~\ref{sec:complexity} compares the resulting procedures.

The allocation $\chi^{\mathrm{OW}}$ is stable but not efficient, overcommitting
$v(N)$ by $\omega^{\mathrm{LR}}$. Spreading that excess evenly over the members,
as in the correspondence of Section~\ref{sec:prelim}, gives the allocation we propose.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:eps}
Let $\chi^{\mathrm{LR}}_{j}:=\chi_j^{\mathrm{OW}}-\omega^{\mathrm{LR}}/n$ for every
$j\in N$. Then $\chi^{\mathrm{LR}}$ is efficient and lies in the weak
$\varepsilon$-core of $\Gamma^{\mathrm{MIP}}$ with
$\varepsilon^{\mathrm{LR}}:=\omega^{\mathrm{LR}}/n$.
\end{corollary}

Practical value of $\chi^{\mathrm{LR}}$ relies on the size of
$\epsilon^{\mathrm{LR}}$, which is determined by $\omega^{\mathrm{LR}}$. 

\subsection{Bound of the Deviation Tolerance $\varepsilon^{LR}$}
\label{sec:eps}

$\omega^{\mathrm{LR}}$ is the welfare the community would gain if each member
could run a fractional schedule instead of committing to a single one of its own. For $j\in N$ let
\begin{equation}
S_j:=\big\{\big(A_jx_j,\ f_j(x_j,y_j)\big):(x_j,y_j)\in\mathcal{X}_j^{\mathrm{MIP}}\big\}
\subset\R^{m+1}
\label{eq:tuple}
\end{equation}
collect the linking contributions and objective values available to prosumer $j$, let
$S_0:=\{(A_0x_0,c_0^{\top}x_0):x_0\ge0\}$, and let $S:=\ssum_{j\in N\cup\{0\}}S_j$.
Since $b_j=0$ in every row, a point $(a,w)\in S$ is realizable by the grand coalition
exactly when $u\le0$, so $\vdp{N}=\max\{w:(a,w)\in S,\ a\le0\}$. Convex combination
commutes with Minkowski summation, so $\conv(S)=\ssum_j\conv(S_j)$ and the same
maximization over $\conv(S)$ returns $\mathrm{val}(\mathrm{DWR}_N)$. A point of $\conv(S_j)$ outside $S_j$ is the image of a fractional schedule.

The constant $\bar\gamma$ is the largest loss a single prosumer can cause the
community by running one of its own schedules rather than a fractional one. It is
fixed by the assets that prosumer may own, and is zero for one that owns no committed
conversion unit, which never needs a fractional schedule. The bound then rests on the
fact that at most $m+1$ prosumers must give up a fractional schedule at an optimum of
$(\mathrm{DWR}_N)$, a count set by the number of linking rows rather than by the
number of members.

\begin{proposition}[Size-decreasing deviation tolerance of proposed allocation]
\label{prop:eps}
Under Assumption~\ref{as:pool}, there is a constant $\bar\gamma$ determined by the
assets a single prosumer may own, and in particular independent of $n$, such that
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon^{\mathrm{LR}}\ \le\ \frac{(m+1)\,\bar\gamma}{n}\ =\ O\!\left(\frac{|T|}{n}\right).
\label{eq:eps_bound}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Fix $j$ and $\bar x_j\in\conv(\mathcal{X}_j^{\mathrm{MIP}})$, and write
$\bar a_j^{\mathrm{bal}}$, $\bar w_j$, $\bar r_{jt}^{u},\bar r_{jt}^{d}$ and
$\bar n_{jt}^{E}$ for its net community position, objective value, reserve
contributions and net market import. Let $\mathcal{X}_j(\bar a_j^{\mathrm{bal}})$ be
the schedules of $j$ whose net community position is $\bar a_j^{\mathrm{bal}}$. It is
nonempty, since any schedule may keep its physical and commitment variables, take
community trades realizing $\bar a_j^{\mathrm{bal}}$, and close
\eqref{eq:pb_E}--\eqref{eq:pb_GH} with its market trades, which \eqref{eq:bnd_size}
admits. Writing $w_j(x)$, $r_{jt}^{u}(x),r_{jt}^{d}(x)$ and $n_{jt}^{E}(x)$ for the
same quantities at $x$, put
\begin{align*}
&L_j(x):=\big(\bar w_j-w_j(x)\big)^{+}\\
&+\Pi^{res}\max_{t\in T}\max\big\{\big(\bar r_{jt}^{u}-r_{jt}^{u}(x)\big)^{+},\,
                       \big(\bar r_{jt}^{d}-r_{jt}^{d}(x)\big)^{+}\big\}\\
&+\delta^{peak}\max_{t}\big(n_{jt}^{E}(x)-\bar n_{jt}^{E}\big)^{+}
\end{align*}
and
\begin{align}
&\tilde\gamma_j:=\!\!\sup_{\bar x_j\in\conv(\mathcal{X}_j^{\mathrm{MIP}})}
\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}_j(\bar a_j^{\mathrm{bal}})}\! L_j(x),\quad
\bar\gamma:=\max_{j\in N}\tilde\gamma_j.
\label{eq:gammatilde}
\end{align}
Each quantity in $L_j$ is a coordinate of a point of $S_j$ or of $\conv(S_j)$, and
$S_j$ is bounded by \eqref{eq:bnd_trade}, so $\tilde\gamma_j$ is finite and depends on
the assets of $j$ alone. If $j$ owns no committed conversion unit then
$\bar x_j\in\mathcal{X}_j(\bar a_j^{\mathrm{bal}})$ and $\tilde\gamma_j=0$.

We exhibit a dispatch that $(\mathrm{DP}_N)$ admits and whose value falls short of
$\mathrm{val}(\mathrm{DWR}_N)$ by at most $(m+1)\bar\gamma$. Let $(\bar a,\bar
w)\in\conv(S)$ with $\bar a\le0$ attain $\mathrm{val}(\mathrm{DWR}_N)$, which is
possible because each $S_j$ is bounded, so that $\bar w=\vdp{N}+\omega^{\mathrm{LR}}$
by Proposition~\ref{prop:opap}. The \emph{Shapley--Folkman}
lemma~\cite{bertsekas2009convex} in $\R^{m+1}$ writes $(\bar a,\bar
w)=\ssum_j(\bar a_j,\bar w_j)$ with $(\bar a_j,\bar w_j)\in\conv(S_j)$ for every $j$
and in $S_j$ for all but at most $m+1$ of them, which we collect in $J$. Since $S_0$
is convex, $J\subseteq N$. Take, for $j\notin J$, the point of
$\mathcal{X}_j^{\mathrm{MIP}}$ realizing $(\bar a_j,\bar w_j)$, and for $j\in J$ a
minimizer $x_j^{\star}$ of $L_j$ over $\mathcal{X}_j(\bar a_j^{\mathrm{bal}})$, and set
$r_i^{sym}$ and $p$ to the values \eqref{eq:dp_res}--\eqref{eq:dp_peak} then allow,
which they do because these rows bound $x_0$ on one side only. Every prosumer keeps
its net community position, so \eqref{eq:dp_bal} holds exactly and the profile is
feasible.

Against the optimal solution of $(\mathrm{DWR}_N)$ nothing changes outside $J$, while
for $j\in J$ the objective value falls by at most $(\bar w_j-w_j(x_j^{\star}))^{+}$,
each reserve sum falls by at most $\max_{t\in T}\max\{(\bar
r_{jt}^{u}-r_{jt}^{u}(x_j^{\star}))^{+},(\bar
r_{jt}^{d}-r_{jt}^{d}(x_j^{\star}))^{+}\}$ and the peak rises by at most
$\max_t(n_{jt}^{E}(x_j^{\star})-\bar n_{jt}^{E})^{+}$. The block prices sum to
$\Pi^{res}$, so the community loses at most $\ssum_{j\in J}L_j(x_j^{\star})\le
(m+1)\bar\gamma$ in all. Since $\vdp{N}$ is the maximum over feasible profiles,
$\vdp{N}\ge\bar w-(m+1)\bar\gamma$, that is $\omega^{\mathrm{LR}}\le(m+1)\bar\gamma$.
Dividing by $n$ and using $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{LR}}=\omega^{\mathrm{LR}}/n$ gives
\eqref{eq:eps_bound}, whose last equality holds because $m=(2|K|+3)|T|$ with $|K|$
fixed.
\end{IEEEproof}

The same bound applies to the \emph{least} deviation tolerance of the game regardless of our proposed allocation.

\begin{corollary}[Size-decreasing the least deviation tolerance]
\label{cor:cos}
Under Assumption~\ref{as:pool}, the cost of stability~\eqref{eq:excess} of
$\Gamma^{\mathrm{MIP}}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\omega^{*}\ \le\ \omega^{\mathrm{LR}}\ \le\ (m+1)\,\bar\gamma ,
\label{eq:cos_bound}
\end{equation}
and the least deviation tolerance~\eqref{eq:eps_star} of $\Gamma^{\mathrm{MIP}}$ obeys
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon^{*}\ \le\ \varepsilon^{\mathrm{LR}}\ \le\ \frac{(m+1)\bar\gamma}{n}
\ =\ O\!\left(\frac{|T|}{n}\right).
\label{eq:eps_star_bound}
\end{equation}
$\mathcal{C}(\vmip)\neq\emptyset$ whenever
$\mathrm{val}(\mathrm{DWR}_N)=\vdp{N}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{IEEEproof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:opap}, $\chi^{\mathrm{OW}}$ is feasible for $(\mathrm{OPAP})$
and sums to $\mathrm{val}(\mathrm{DWR}_N)$, so
$\alpha^{*}\le\mathrm{val}(\mathrm{DWR}_N)$ and $\omega^{*}\le\omega^{\mathrm{LR}}$.
The remaining inequality is Proposition~\ref{prop:eps}, and dividing by $n$ with
$\varepsilon^{*}=\omega^{*}/n$ gives the second display.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{remark}[Extension to Stochastic Modeling]
\label{rmk:stoch}
Accounting for stochasticity of forecast error does not disturb the result this paper rests on,
that the deviation tolerance decays in the community size. Let $\Omega$ be a
finite scenario set, where the electrolyzer states and the reserve capacities
are decided before the realization and the flexible assets are redispatched
after it. The operating region $\mathcal{X}_j^{\mathrm{MIP}}$ is then the
scenario-expanded set of non-anticipative plans of prosumer $j$, again bounded
and again of the form~\eqref{eq:Xmip}, and the linking
constraints~\eqref{eq:dp_bal}--\eqref{eq:dp_peak}, imposed in every scenario,
remain additive on both sides. Assumption~\ref{as:pool} therefore holds and
Section~\ref{sec:duality} carries over unchanged: the sub-game is again a linear
production game, and $\chi^{\mathrm{LR}}$ is again read off the dual of a single
grand-coalition master. The stand-alone values $\kappa_j$ are then the scenario-expanded
single-member problems, and $\Gamma^{\mathrm{MIP}}$ is normalized by them
as in \eqref{eq:v0}.

Only the constant of Proposition~\ref{prop:eps} moves. The linking rows number
$m=(2|K|+3)|T||\Omega|$, whereas $\bar\gamma$ is unaffected by $|\Omega|$: the
objective enters \eqref{eq:gammatilde} as an expectation, and the reserve and
peak terms enter through capacities that no scenario count alters. Uncertainty
thus raises the tolerance that a community of a given size must accept, and
leaves untouched the rate at which that tolerance vanishes as the community
grows.
\end{remark}

 
\section{Solution Procedures and Complexity}
\label{sec:complexity}

We compare the computation of $\chi^{\mathrm{LR}}$ with that of the exact row
generation that is the standard benchmark. Neither is polynomial, since evaluating
$v(S)$ once already embeds a unit-commitment dispatch, which is in general strongly
NP-hard~\cite{bendotti2019complexity}. Both decompose into a master linear program and
a subproblem, so the comparison is between the two subproblems.

\emph{Row generation for $(\mathrm{OPAP})$}~\cite{drechsel2010computing}\footnote{The
same procedure carries two names. Solving $(\mathrm{OPAP})$ in primal form generates
the coalition inequalities as \emph{rows}, while solving its dual generates coalitions as
\emph{columns}~\cite{caprara2010new,liu2016computing}. We use the former throughout, to keep
the procedure distinct from the column generation that solves $(\mathrm{DWR}_N)$.} maintains a
subset of the coalition inequalities, solves the restricted $(\mathrm{OPAP})$ for an
incumbent $\chi$, and appends a violated inequality found by separating
\begin{equation}
\max_{S\subseteq N}\ \Big\{v(S)-\ssum_{j\in S}\chi_j\Big\}
\label{eq:rg_sep}
\end{equation}
A nonpositive value certifies that $\chi$ solves $(\mathrm{OPAP})$ exactly. The
separation encodes coalition membership by $n$ selection binaries that enter the
dispatch through big-$M$ constraints deactivating the variables of excluded
prosumers~\cite[Sec.~3]{drechsel2010computing}. It is a single monolithic MILP that
stacks a subset-selection layer on an already combinatorial dispatch, grows in $n$ on
both layers, admits no decomposition across prosumers, and has a relaxation weakened
by the big-$M$ constants.

\emph{Column generation for $(\mathrm{DWR}_N)$}. The master is $(\mathrm{DWR}_N)$
restricted to a subset of columns, a linear program with $m+n$ rows returning
$(\theta,\sigma)$. Its subproblem prices prosumer $j$,
\begin{equation}
\max_{(x_j,y_j)\in\mathcal{X}_j^{\mathrm{MIP}}}\ f_j(x_j,y_j)-\theta^{\top}A_jx_j,
\label{eq:pricing}
\end{equation}
and adds a column when the value exceeds $\sigma_j$, so $Q_j$ is never enumerated. It
decomposes into $n$ independent single-prosumer MIPs that can be solved in parallel,
each of the size of one prosumer. Neither procedure admits a bound on its iteration
count. The stand-alone values $\kappa_j$ that \eqref{eq:v0} requires are $n$
single-prosumer MILPs of the size of \eqref{eq:pricing}, solved once.

Table~\ref{tab:complexity} collects the comparison. Read by column, it gives the
trade-off. Row generation attains the least excess $\omega^{*}$ with an oracle that
grows in $n$, while the proposed scheme settles for $\omega^{\mathrm{LR}}$, bounded in
Proposition~\ref{prop:eps}, and buys an oracle whose size does not depend on $n$.
Section~\ref{sec:num} measures both sides of that exchange.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
We instantiate the community of Section~\ref{sec:model} at
$|N|\in\{6,15,30,60\}$ over $|T|=24$ hourly periods. Load and price data are taken
from Jeju, Korea, and wind data from the Danish DK2 bidding zone, both for January.
Both community-level channels are active. A symmetric FCR-N-type reserve product is
sold in six four-hour blocks at $56$~\euro/MW$\cdot$h, and a coincident-peak charge of
$150$~\euro/MW applies to the community import peak. The remaining parameters are
reported in Table~S1 of the supplementary material and are held fixed across the four
sizes, so the size axis varies $n$ and nothing else.
 
Community size is varied by adding members, not by replacing them, so each community
contains the smaller one unchanged. At $n=6$ every function is held by a single
archetype and each coalition value can be traced to a named role. The members added at
$n=15$ and $n=30$ put more than one owner on each role and bring in members that own
generation and conversion together, standalone storage operators, and consumers active
in two carriers. The $n=60$ community doubles every asset count at $n=30$, but each
added member carries its own capacity multiplier and is not a copy of the one it
mirrors. This matters, because a two-fold replica would have a core that shrinks toward
the competitive allocation by the Debreu--Scarf theorem, and the trend measured across
sizes would be an artefact of replication. What is held constant along the size axis is
the share of members that carry commitment binaries, which is $33.3\%$, $40.0\%$,
$33.3\%$ and $33.3\%$ at the four sizes. These members are the source of the
integrality gap $\omega^{\mathrm{LR}}$, so the behaviour of $\omega^{\mathrm{LR}}$ in
$n$ is not confounded by a non-convex share that drifts with $n$. Tables~S2--S5 of the
supplementary material report the composition of the four communities and the asset
matrix of every member.
 
Each size is run over the $31$ days of January, one instance per day, and the
aggregates reported below are geometric means, which is the appropriate summary for a
quantity whose scaling in $n$ is the object of interest. The dispatch MILP, the
column-generation master, and all pricing and separation problems are solved with SCIP.
The row-generation separation \eqref{eq:rg_sep} is solved with Gurobi under a time
budget of $3600$~s. Timings are wall-clock values measured with one solve running at a
time.

\subsection{Stability of the Proposed Allocation}
Table~\ref{tab:results} compares the bound $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{LR}}$, available before any
coalition is examined, with the actual per-capita excess. For a \emph{fixed}
allocation $\chi$ that maximum per-capita excess is
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon(\chi)=\max_{\emptyset\neq S\subsetneq N}\ \frac{v(S)-\ssum_{j\in S}\chi_j}{|S|},
\label{eq:pcexcess}
\end{equation}
which is the separation objective \eqref{eq:rg_sep} scaled by $|S|$. It certifies
$\chi\in\mathcal{C}(v)$ when nonpositive and confirms the weak $\varepsilon$-core membership
of Proposition~\ref{prop:eps} when it does not exceed $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{LR}}$. As a
mixed-integer fractional program, \eqref{eq:pcexcess} is evaluated by Dinkelbach's
method~\cite{dinkelbach1967nonlinear}, each iteration a single MILP.

The duality gap $\omega^{\mathrm{LR}}$ is between $7.0$ and $11.1$~\euro{} at every size
tested, showing no trend as membership grows from $6$ to $60$. This is the behavior
Proposition~\ref{prop:eps} rests on, and $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{LR}}$, which divides that gap
by $n$, accordingly falls from $1.37$ to $0.12$~\euro. The measured
$\varepsilon(\chi^{LR})$ stays under it at every size, so the bound proposed in Proposition~\ref{prop:eps} holds on all instances. At
$n=6$ it is zero, so $\chi^{LR}$ lies in the exact core. At $n=60$ calculating $\varepsilon(\chi^{LR})$ itself becomes intractable, terminating within 3,600 seconds on $18$ of the $31$ days, with
the remaining $13$ returning no verdict.

\subsection{Computational Scalability}
Table~\ref{tab:runtime} compares the computation time the proposed scheme takes with that of
the exact row generation it replaces. The proposed scheme requires one grand-coalition MILP and $n$ stand-alone value MILPs,
which any allocation rule has to solve in order to know $v(N)$, and one column-generation
solve on top of it. Since the latter is decomposable into $n$ pricing MILPs per iteration, its
time grows moderately as $n$ goes from $6$ to $60$, from $28.4$ to $164.8$~s.

Row generation moves in the opposite direction, and the contrast is what
Proposition~\ref{prop:opap}(ii) asserts. It is the cheaper method at $n=6$, certifying every
instance in $6.4$~s, and still certifies every instance at $n=15$, but at $886$~s, more than
eleven times the column generation it would replace. At $n=30$ and $n=60$ it returns no
certificate on any of the $31$ instances within an hour. The obstruction is the separation
oracle \eqref{eq:rg_sep}, which selects a coalition through $n$ binaries with big-$M$
deactivation on top of an already combinatorial dispatch and admits no decomposition, so it
grows on both layers at once. That oracle replaces the explicit enumeration of all $2^{n}-1$ coalition constraints, each of
which requires an MILP solve to evaluate $\vmip(S)$, with the count given in the last row of
the table. The copositive program \eqref{eq:cpp} serves only as a theoretical device for establishing
nonemptiness of the core and does not replace row generation, exhausting the same budget
already at $n=6$.

  \subsection{Fairness of the Proposed Allocation}
\label{sec:fair}
Core stability reflects \emph{group fairness}, what is being fair allocation has various meanings and axioms. Therefore cooperative game theory investigated other solution concepts such as shapley value, egalitarian allocation, and mix of them. Taking egalitarianism as a secondary norm, Fioriti \emph{et al.}~\cite{fioriti2024fair}
select the core element closest to the equal split, on the ground that the surplus is a
product of the community as a whole and no member has a prior claim to a larger share of it. Writing
$V(\chi)=\ssum_{j\in N}(\chi_j-\bar\chi)^{2}$ with $\bar\chi=\ssum_j\chi_j/n$,
which for an efficient allocation is the squared distance to the equal split,
this \emph{variance core} is
$\chi^{\mathrm{VC}}=\arg\min\{V(\chi)\,\mid\,\chi\in\mathcal{C}(v)\}$. It is
computed by running the same row generation of Section~\ref{sec:complexity} with replacing
$V(\chi)$ as the master objective function.

Table~\ref{tab:fair} reports $V(\chi^{LR})$ against $V(\chi^{\mathrm{VC}})$, the smallest
variance any core element attains. Both are measured on the shares of the surplus $v(N)$,
so the equal split they are compared against gives every member $v(N)/n$ over and above
its stand-alone value $\kappa_j$. At $n=6$ the proposed allocation carries eight times
that minimum, and its worst-placed member sits $215$~\euro{} from where the egalitarian
criterion would put it, against a mean share of $183$~\euro. Dispersion is precisely what
a duality allocation is indifferent to, and at this size it gives up most of it. At $n=15$
the difference collapses: the allocation is within $16\%$ of the minimum on average and
never worse than $34\%$ on any day, and its worst-displaced member is $28$~\euro{} away on
a mean share of $138$~\euro. A plausible reason is that the
larger community admits substitutable prosumers. At $n=6$ each asset class has a single owner and no
member can be replaced, whereas at $n=15$ each has two to four owners of comparable or
identical capacity. That result does not transfer directly, since our members are
not exact duplicates and the game is not an LP. It is suggestive nonetheless, because
$\sigma(\theta^{*})$ is the Owen point of the Lagrangian sub-game by Lemma~\ref{lem:lpg} and
the game separates from that sub-game only by $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{LR}}$, which shrinks with
$n$.

Since the variance core exploits a row-generation scheme, it inherits the limits of
Section~\ref{sec:complexity}. It remains tractable in~\cite{fioriti2024fair} because the
underlying model is linear there, whereas here it fails to converge within the budget at
$n=30$ and $n=60$, so no comparison is available at those
sizes. The proposed allocation therefore combines scalability with an egalitarian fairness
close to what the variance core attains.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}
 
We have studied stable profit allocation in a sector-coupled energy community whose dispatch
model is non-convex. Members of such a community are coupled only through constraints that are
additive on both sides, and we exploited this structure to obtain an allocation from the dual
of a single grand-coalition solve. It is stable against every coalition and relaxes efficiency
by the duality gap alone, which yields a per-member deviation tolerance of $O(|T|/n)$, and the
same argument applied to an exact copositive reformulation removes the tolerance altogether and
gives conditions under which the core of the mixed-integer community game is nonempty.
Experiments on communities of up to 60 prosumers confirmed the scalability and the predicted
decay, and a comparison against the variance core showed that the egalitarian fairness forgone
diminishes as the community grows. Since the tolerance divides the duality gap by the number of
members, and the gap is set by the constraints that link them rather than by how many of them
there are, non-convex assets pose less of an obstacle to cohesion in larger communities than in
smaller ones.

Several questions are left for future research. One is profit allocation over multiple
decision stages under uncertainty, which Remark~\ref{rmk:stoch} touches on. Another is
relaxing Assumption~\ref{as:pool}, network limits being the leading example, which
Remark~\ref{rmk:nonadditive} indicates would call for a different treatment.
