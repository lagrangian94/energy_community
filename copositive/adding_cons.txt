# Two Additional Homogeneous Coupling Constraints: Reserve (Up/Down) and Peak Penalty

**Context.** Beyond the community energy balance (our baseline coupling
constraint), this note formalizes two additional **homogeneous coupling
constraints** — i.e. coupling constraints whose right-hand side is zero once the
shared variable is moved to the left — adapted from the community-market
literature: **frequency reserve provision (asymmetric up/down)** and a
**community peak-power penalty**. Both preserve the linear-production-game (LPG)
structure through an active common-technology block $(A_0, c_0, x_0)$.

Notation follows the preprint: prosumers $j\in N$, carriers $k\in\{E,G,H\}$,
periods $t\in T$, coalition $S\subseteq N$. Market exchange carries superscript
$mkt$, community exchange $com$. Hourly resolution, so $\Delta T = 1$ throughout
(stated as a footnote; power in kW and energy-per-hour in kWh coincide
numerically).

All three coupling constraints (balance, reserve, peak) are **homogeneous** in the
sense that their right-hand side is $0 = \sum_{j\in S} 0$: no coalition member
endows the constraint. In the LPG template
$$
v(S)=\max_{x_j, x_0\ge 0}\Bigl\{\sum_{j\in S}c_j^\top x_j + c_0^\top x_0
:\ \sum_{j\in S}A_j x_j + A_0 x_0 \le \sum_{j\in S}b_j\Bigr\},
$$
these rows have $b_j = 0$, with any shared decision carried by the
common-technology block $(A_0, c_0, x_0)$.

---

## 1. Baseline recap: the balance coupling

$$
\sum_{j\in S}\bigl(i_{jt}^{k,com} - e_{jt}^{k,com}\bigr) = 0,
\qquad \forall t\in T,\ \forall k\in\{E,G,H\}.
\tag{C0}
$$
This is the $b_j = 0$, $A_0 = \mathbf 0$ case: common technology inactive. Reserve
and peak **activate** $(A_0, c_0, x_0)$.

---

## 2. Coupling constraint I — frequency reserve (asymmetric up/down)

### 2.1 Design decision (justified by the literature scan)
Reserve is a **single electricity-side service** sold to the grid. There is **no
hydrogen or heat reserve** as a traded ancillary service (hydrogen is buffered by
line-pack; heat by thermal inertia; neither is bid as an ancillary product). Every
sector-coupling asset — electric storage, electrolyzer, heat pump — contributes to
the *same* power reserve. Hence reserve variables carry **no carrier index**.

Following the electrolyzer-reserve literature (Saretta et al. 2023; Samani et al.
2020), we split reserve into **separate upward and downward products** rather than
a single symmetric one, since sector-coupling loads have asymmetric headroom
(a partially-loaded electrolyzer can shed or add different amounts of power).
The two community-wide variables are
$$
r^{up}\ge 0 \quad(\text{sold at }\pi^{up}),
\qquad
r^{dn}\ge 0 \quad(\text{sold at }\pi^{dn}).
$$

### 2.2 Per-prosumer reserve headroom (stays inside $\mathcal X_j$)
Let $r_{jt}^{+}, r_{jt}^{-}\ge 0$ be prosumer $j$'s aggregate up/down reserve
offered at $t$ (summed over its storage, electrolyzer, heat pump). Each asset's
contribution is bounded by its own power and energy headroom, using only
prosumer $j$'s variables.

**Electric storage** (state of charge $s_{jt}^E$, charge/discharge
$b_{jt}^{E,ch}, b_{jt}^{E,dis}$, efficiencies $\nu_j^{E,ch}, \nu_j^{E,dis}$):
$$
\bigl(b_{jt}^{E,dis}-b_{jt}^{E,ch}\bigr) + r_{jt}^{+,\mathrm{sto}}
\le \overline b_j^{E,dis},
\qquad
\bigl(b_{jt}^{E,ch}-b_{jt}^{E,dis}\bigr) + r_{jt}^{-,\mathrm{sto}}
\le \overline b_j^{E,ch},
$$
$$
r_{jt}^{+,\mathrm{sto}}\le \nu_j^{E,dis}\bigl(s_{jt}^E-\underline s_j^E\bigr),
\qquad
r_{jt}^{-,\mathrm{sto}}\le \tfrac{1}{\nu_j^{E,ch}}\bigl(\overline s_j^E-s_{jt}^E\bigr).
$$

**Electrolyzer** (a load: upward reserve = reduce consumption toward its minimum;
downward = increase toward capacity), gated by the on/standby state:
$$
\tilde d_{jt}^{E\to G,sc} - r_{jt}^{+,\mathrm{els}}
\ge \underline d^{E\to G,sc} z_{jt}^{G,on} + c^{G,lb} z_{jt}^{G,sb},
$$
$$
\tilde d_{jt}^{E\to G,sc} + r_{jt}^{-,\mathrm{els}}
\le \overline d^{E\to G,sc} z_{jt}^{G,on} + c^{G,lb} z_{jt}^{G,sb}.
$$

**Heat pump** (same load logic, gated by its on-state):
$$
d_{jt}^{E\to H,sc} - r_{jt}^{+,\mathrm{hp}}
\ge \underline c^H\,\overline d^{E\to H,sc} z_{jt}^{H,on},
\qquad
d_{jt}^{E\to H,sc} + r_{jt}^{-,\mathrm{hp}}
\le \overline c^H\,\overline d^{E\to H,sc} z_{jt}^{H,on}.
$$

Aggregating per prosumer,
$r_{jt}^{+} := r_{jt}^{+,\mathrm{sto}}+r_{jt}^{+,\mathrm{els}}+r_{jt}^{+,\mathrm{hp}}$
and likewise $r_{jt}^{-}$. **All rows above are prosumer-private** — they enter
$A_j$, never $A_0$.

### 2.3 The coupling rows (activate the common variables)
Each sold reserve product cannot exceed what the coalition can deliver in **every**
hour (the scarcest hour binds), independently for up and down:
$$
r^{up} \le \sum_{j\in S} r_{jt}^{+}, \qquad \forall t\in T,
\tag{C-Res$\uparrow$}
$$
$$
r^{dn} \le \sum_{j\in S} r_{jt}^{-}, \qquad \forall t\in T.
\tag{C-Res$\downarrow$}
$$
Moving the shared variables left gives $r^{up} - \sum_{j\in S} r_{jt}^{+}\le 0
= \sum_{j\in S}0$ (and similarly for $r^{dn}$): homogeneous, with $x_0 \ni
(r^{up}, r^{dn})$, $A_0$ carrying $+1$ on the respective shared variable, member
coefficients $-1$, and $b_j = 0$.

### 2.4 Objective
Add $+\pi^{up} r^{up} + \pi^{dn} r^{dn}$ (components of $c_0$, positive signs).

---

## 3. Coupling constraint II — community peak penalty

### 3.1 Variable and constraint
The community pays $\delta^{peak}$ per unit of peak net grid import $p\ge 0$
(electricity, $k=E$):
$$
\sum_{j\in S}\bigl(i_{jt}^{E,mkt} - e_{jt}^{E,mkt}\bigr) \le p, \qquad \forall t\in T.
\tag{C-Peak}
$$
Moving $p$ left: $\sum_{j\in S}(i_{jt}^{E,mkt}-e_{jt}^{E,mkt}) - p \le 0
= \sum_{j\in S}0$: homogeneous, $x_0 \ni p$, $A_0$ carrying $-1$ on $p$, member
coefficients $\pm 1$ on grid exchange, $b_j = 0$. Since $p$ carries only the
negative cost $-\delta^{peak}p$, at optimum $p = \max_t \sum_{j\in S}
(i_{jt}^{E,mkt}-e_{jt}^{E,mkt})$, the coalition's net-import peak.

### 3.2 Objective
Add $-\delta^{peak} p$ (a component of $c_0$, **negative** sign).

### 3.3 Where the netting benefit lives
Net import is summed over members *before* the max, so simultaneous
import/export of different prosumers partially cancels — entirely within the
coalition sum on the LHS of a single row, with no cross-prosumer term in $A_0$.
Fully LPG-compatible.

---

## 4. Combined common-technology block

$$
x_0 = \bigl(r^{up},\, r^{dn},\, p\bigr)^\top \ge 0,
\qquad
c_0 = \bigl(+\pi^{up},\, +\pi^{dn},\, -\delta^{peak}\bigr)^\top.
$$

| Row (per $t$) | $r^{up}$ | $r^{dn}$ | $p$ | member coeff | RHS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| balance (C0) | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $i^{com}-e^{com}$ (eq.) | $0$ |
| reserve up (C-Res$\uparrow$) | $+1$ | $0$ | $0$ | $-r_{jt}^{+}$ | $0$ |
| reserve down (C-Res$\downarrow$) | $0$ | $+1$ | $0$ | $-r_{jt}^{-}$ | $0$ |
| peak (C-Peak) | $0$ | $0$ | $-1$ | $i^{mkt}-e^{mkt}$ | $0$ |

Everything else is inside $\mathcal X_j$.

---

## 5. Why the reduction survives

Dual of the grand-coalition problem:
$$
\min_\mu\ \mu^\top\!\Bigl(\sum_{j\in N}b_j\Bigr)
\ \ \text{s.t.}\ \ A_j^\top\mu\ge c_j\ \forall j\in N,\ \
A_0^\top\mu\ge c_0,\ \ \mu\ge 0.
$$
Let $\mu^*$ be optimal, $\chi_j := (\mu^*)^\top b_j$. For any $S\subseteq N$, the
dual $(\mathrm D_S)$ has constraint set
$\{A_j^\top\mu\ge c_j: j\in S\}\cup\{A_0^\top\mu\ge c_0\}\cup\{\mu\ge0\}$, a
**subset** of $(\mathrm D_N)$'s constraints — the common-block row
$A_0^\top\mu\ge c_0$ appears in *both*, unchanged, because it has no $j$-index.
Hence $\mu^*$ is feasible for $(\mathrm D_S)$, and by weak duality on the bounded
coalition LP,
$$
\sum_{j\in S}\chi_j = (\mu^*)^\top\!\sum_{j\in S}b_j \ge v(S)\quad\forall S,
\qquad \sum_{j\in N}\chi_j = v(N),
$$
so $\chi$ is in the core. The sign of $c_0$ is irrelevant (reserve's $+\pi^{up},
+\pi^{dn}$ and peak's $-\delta^{peak}$ are all fine); the argument uses only the
coalition-invariance of the homogeneous common-block row $A_0^\top\mu\ge c_0$.

## 6. Referee-facing caveats (rational, not optimistic)

1. **Coalition-invariant tariffs are a standing assumption, not a fact.** The
   common technology requires $c_0, A_0$ independent of $S$. A *community-level*
   peak or reserve scheme could push the DSO/TSO to reprice with community size; if
   any of $\delta^{peak}, \pi^{up}, \pi^{dn}$ scaled with $S$, then $c_0$ would be
   $S$-dependent and the §5 subset argument would fail. State fixed-tariff as an
   explicit hypothesis and flag its limitation.

2. **Reserve introduces variables the current numerics do not exercise.** The
   scenario data carry no reserve prices or requirements. If reserve enters the
   manuscript as more than a structural extension, at least one worked instance
   must include it; otherwise a referee asks "where is the experiment?". A
   defensible middle course: present reserve as a *structural extension* (this
   note) plus one illustrative case, rather than re-running all scenarios with
   assumed reserve prices that invite "why these values?".

3. **Reserve/peak are LPG-safe only in the linear relaxation.** With UC binaries
   ($z^{G,on}, z^{G,sb}, z^{H,on}$, startup), the electrolyzer/heat-pump reserve
   headroom rows in §2.2 couple to the commitment variables, so $v(S)$ becomes
   mixed-integer, the LPG structure is lost, and the paper's non-convex pricing
   (IP/CHP) is what delivers a near-core allocation. This is consistent with — and
   reinforces — the thesis that **integrality, not the reserve/peak coupling per
   se, is the source of difficulty.** Reserve/peak are "free" only where the model
   is already linear; do not oversell them.

4. **Up/down doubles the shared-variable count and the market-data burden.**
   Relative to a symmetric product, the asymmetric split adds a second common
   variable and a second price series to justify. It buys a tighter representation
   of asymmetric sector-coupling headroom, but that gain is orthogonal to the
   paper's stability contribution. Worth a sentence acknowledging the trade-off,
   since a referee may ask whether the extra product is load-bearing for the
   paper's claims (it is not — the symmetric version yields the same LPG/core
   result with one fewer variable).
